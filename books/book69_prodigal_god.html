The Prodigal God: Two Lost Sons and a Reckless Father

Little Believers Tigrinya Bible Stories

Ages 10-17

Written by Daniel Tesfamariam

Key Verse: Luke 15:24 — "For this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found."

Jesus had a habit that infuriated religious people: He ate with sinners. Not just tolerated them — He celebrated with them. Tax collectors who cheated the poor. Women with ruined reputations. Men who had abandoned the faith. They all crowded around Jesus, and He welcomed every one of them.

The Pharisees and teachers of the law muttered, "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them." They meant it as an insult. But Jesus heard it as the highest compliment. He was about to tell three stories that would forever reveal the heart of God — and expose the hidden lostness of religious people.

What follows in Luke 15 is not just a parable. It is the gospel in miniature — the most important chapter Jesus ever taught.

የሱስ ንሃይማኖተኛታት ዘቖጥዕ ልምዲ ነበሮ፡ ምስ ሓጥኣን ይበልዕ ነበረ። ጻውር ጥራይ ዘይኮነ — ምሳታቶም ይሕጐስ ነበረ። ንድኻታት ዝሰረቑ ተቐበልቲ ቀረጽ፡ ዝበላሸወ ስም ዘለወን ኣንስቲ፡ ንእምነቶም ዝሓደጉ ሰብኡት — ኵሎም ኣብ ዙርያ የሱስ ተኣኪቦም ንኵሎም ይቕበሎም ነበረ።

ፈሪሳውያንን መምህራን ሕግን ኣጒረምረሙ፡ "እዚ ሰብኣይ ንሓጥኣን ይቕበሎም ምሳታቶም ከኣ ይበልዕ።" ከም ጸርፊ ማለቶም ነበረ። የሱስ ግና ከም ዝለዓለ ውዳሰ ሰምዖ። ንልቢ ኣምላኽ ንዘልኣለም ዝገልጹ ሰለስተ ዛንታታት ክነግር ይዳሎ ነበረ — ንሕቡእ ጥፍኣት ሃይማኖተኛታት ድማ ክቃልዕ።

(Yesus ni haymanotenyatat zeqot'i limdi nebero: mis hat'an yibel'i nebere. Tsawir teray zeyikone — misatatom yihgos nebere.)
Scene 2
[PICTURE: A shepherd in the wilderness, leaving 99 sheep in a green pasture, climbing over rocky hills in the dark with a lantern, searching desperately for one lost sheep. His face shows determination and love, not anger. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

"Suppose one of you has a hundred sheep and loses one of them," Jesus began. "Doesn't he leave the ninety-nine in the open country and go after the lost one until he finds it?"

Think about how reckless this sounds. What sensible businessman risks ninety-nine to find one? The math doesn't work. But love doesn't do math. The shepherd doesn't sit and wait for the sheep to find its way back. He goes. He searches. He doesn't stop until that one lost sheep is found.

And when he finds it? He doesn't scold it. He doesn't drag it back by force. He joyfully puts it on his shoulders and carries it home. Then he throws a party — a celebration bigger than the value of one sheep. This is who God is. He is the searching God.

"ካባኻትኩም ሓደ ሚእቲ ኣባጊዕ ዘለዎ ሓንቲ ኣባ ጽጉ እንተ ኣጥፊኡ ነተን ተስዓን ትሽዓተን ኣብ ግዳም ሓዲጉ ነታ ዝጠፍአት ክሳዕ ዝረኽባ ኣይከይድን'ዶ?" ኢሉ የሱስ ጀመረ።

ክንደይ ዘይልቦና ከም ዝመስል ሕሰቡ። ኣየናይ ለባም ነጋዳይ ንሓንቲ ኢሉ ንተስዓን ትሽዓተን ይጻገም? ሒሳቡ ኣይኸይድን። ፍቕሪ ግና ሒሳብ ኣይገብርን። እቲ ጓሳ ኮፍ ኢሉ እታ በጊዕ ክትምለስ ኣይጽበን። ይኸይድ። ይደሊ። ክሳዕ እታ ሓንቲ ጥፍእቲ በጊዕ ትርከብ ኣይተርፍን።

ምስ ረኸባ ድማ? ኣይዝልፋን። ብሓይሊ ኣይጐተታን። ብሓጐስ ኣብ መንኩቡ ኣቐሚጡ ናብ ገዝኡ ይጾራ። ድሕሪኡ ድማ በዓል የብዕል — ካብ ዋጋ ሓንቲ በጊዕ ዝዓቢ ምብዓል። ኣምላኽ ከምዚ እዩ። ንሱ ዝደሊ ኣምላኽ እዩ።

(Kabakhatikum hade mi'iti abagi'i zelewo hanti aba ts'igu inte at'fi'u neten tes'an tish'aten ab gidam hadigu neta zetefeat k'isab zirekba aykeydndo?)
Scene 3
[PICTURE: A woman on her hands and knees in a dark room, sweeping every corner with a broom, holding a small oil lamp close to the ground. Her face is intense with determination. "Or suppose a woman has ten silver coins and loses one," Jesus continued. "Doesn't she light a lamp, sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it?"

These ten coins were likely her marriage dowry — her entire financial security. Losing one wasn't carelessness; it was crisis. So she tears the house apart. She moves every piece of furniture. She sweeps every crack. She doesn't rest until that coin is found.

And when she finds it? She calls her friends and neighbors: "Rejoice with me! I have found my lost coin!" Again, a celebration that seems disproportionate. One coin. A whole party. But this is the point: "In the same way, there is rejoicing in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents." Heaven itself throws a party for one lost person found.

"ወይ ሓንቲ ሰበይቲ ዓሰርተ ብሩር ሳንቲም ዘለዋ ሓንቲ እንተ ኣጥፊኣ መብራህቲ ኣብሪሃ ነቲ ገዛ ጸሪጋ ክሳዕ ትረኽባ ብጥንቃቐ ኣይትደልን'ዶ?" ኢሉ የሱስ ቀጸለ።

እዘን ዓሰርተ ሳንቲም ናይ መርዓኣ ማሕረስ — ምሉእ ቊጠባዊ ውሕስነታ ኮይነን ይኽእላ። ሓንቲ ምጥፋእ ሸለልትነት ኣይነበረን፤ ቅልውላው ነበረ። ስለዚ ነቲ ገዛ ትበትኮ። ኵሉ ኣቕሓ ተንቀሳቕስ። ኵሉ ስንጭሮ ትጸርግ። ክሳዕ እታ ሳንቲም ትርከብ ኣይተርፍን።

ምስ ረኸበታ ድማ? ንኣዕሩኽታን ጐረባብታን ትጽውዕ፡ "ምሳይ ተሓጐሱ! ነታ ዝጠፍአት ሳንቲመይ ረኺበያ!" ከም ብሓድሽ ሓደ ዝበዝሐ ምብዓል። ሓንቲ ሳንቲም። ምሉእ በዓል። ግን ነጥቢ እዚ እዩ፡ "ከምኡ ድማ ሓደ ሓጥእ ምስ ዝንስሕ ኣብ ቅድሚ መላእኽቲ ኣምላኽ ሓጐስ ኣሎ።" ሰማይ ባዕሉ ንሓንቲ ዝጠፍአት ነፍሲ ዝተረኽበት በዓል የብዕል።

(Wey hanti sebeyti aserte birur santim zelebwa hanti inte at'fi'a mebrahti abriha neti geza tseriga k'isab trekba bitinqaqe ayitdelin'do?)
Scene 4
[PICTURE: A young man with a defiant expression standing before his elderly father in a wealthy household. The son's hand is outstretched, demanding. The father's face shows heartbreak but not anger. Other servants look on in shock. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

Now Jesus tells the main story — the one that would shake everyone listening. "There was a man who had two sons. The younger one said to his father, 'Father, give me my share of the estate.'"

In that culture, this request was unspeakable. You only received your inheritance when your father died. By asking for it now, the younger son was essentially saying: "Father, I wish you were dead. You're worth more to me dead than alive. I want your things, but I don't want YOU."

Every person listening would have gasped. In that society, a father would have been within his rights to strike his son, disown him, or have the community publicly shame him. This wasn't just disrespect — it was the deepest possible rejection of a father's love.

ሕጂ የሱስ ነታ ቀንዲ ዛንታ ይነግር — ንኵሎም ዝሰምዑ ዘንቀጥቅጥ። "ክልተ ኣወዳት ዝነበሮ ሓደ ሰብኣይ ነበረ። እቲ ንእሽቶ ነቦኡ 'ኣቦ ግደይ ንብረት ሃበኒ' በሎ።"

ኣብ ቲ ባህሊ እዚ ሕቶ ዘይዝረብ ነበረ። ውርሻኻ ኣቦኻ ምስ ዝሞት ጥራይ ትቕበል። ሕጂ ብምሕታቱ እቲ ንእሽቶ ወዲ ብመሰረቱ ከምዚ ይብል ነበረ፡ "ኣቦ ሞይትካ እንተ ትኸውን ይሓይሽ ነይሩ። ሞይትካ ካብ ብህይውትካ ትበዝሓኒ። ንብረትካ ይደሊ ግን ንዓኻ ኣይደልን።"

ኵሉ ዝሰምዐ ሰብ ኣስተንፊሱ ክኸውን ኣለዎ። ኣብ ቲ ሕብረተሰብ ኣቦ ንወዱ ክዓርፎ፡ ክነጽጎ ወይ ማሕበረሰብ ብጋህዲ ክዅንኖ ክገብር መሰል ነበሮ። ዘይምኽባር ጥራይ ኣይነበረን — ናይ ፍቕሪ ኣቦ ዝዓሞቐ ዝከኣል ንጽጊ ነበረ።

(Hiji Yesus neta qendi zanta yinegir — nkulom zism'u zenqetqit. "Kilte awedat znebero hade seb'ay nebere. Iti ni'ishto ne'abohu 'Abo gidey nibret habeni' belo.")

[PICTURE: The father with tears in his eyes, handing over bags of gold and legal documents to his younger son. The father's hands tremble but he gives freely. The older son watches from a doorway with a cold expression. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

"So he divided his property between them." Just like that. No lecture. No conditions. No guilt trip. The father simply gives.

This is the most shocking line in the story — more shocking than the son's request. Any father in that culture would have refused. But this father gives his son exactly what he asks for. Why? Because real love cannot be forced. Real love must be freely chosen, and that means it must be possible to reject it.

God doesn't hold us prisoner. He doesn't manipulate us into staying. He gives us the terrible, beautiful gift of freedom — even the freedom to walk away from Him. This is not weakness. This is the most courageous kind of love — the kind that risks being rejected rather than forcing compliance.

"ስለዚ ንብረቱ ኣካፈሎም።" ከምኡ ጥራይ። ስብከት የለን። ቅድመ ኵነት የለን። ናይ ሕልና ቅጭ የለን። እቲ ኣቦ ብቐሊሉ ይህብ።

እዚ ኣብ ዛንታ ዝበለጸ ዘስደምም መስመር እዩ — ካብ ሕቶ ወዲ ዝበልጽ ዘስደምም። ኣብ ቲ ባህሊ ዝኾነ ኣቦ ኣይቅበልን ነይሩ። ግን እዚ ኣቦ ንወዱ ዝሓተቶ ብልክዕ ይህቦ። ንምንታይ? ምኽንያቱ ሓቀኛ ፍቕሪ ብግድን ክኸውን ኣይክእልን። ሓቀኛ ፍቕሪ ብናጻ ክምረጽ ኣለዎ፡ እዚ ድማ ክንጸግ ይከኣል ማለት እዩ።

ኣምላኽ ኣሲሩ ኣይሕዘናን። ክንጸንሕ ኣይጐሰናን። ናይ ናጽነት ዘስካሕክሕን ጽቡቕን ህያብ ይህበና — ክንርሕቆ'ውን ናጽነት ሓዊሱ። እዚ ድኻም ኣይኮነን። እዚ ዝበለጸ ጅግንነታዊ ዓይነት ፍቕሪ እዩ — ምግዳድ ካብ ምፍራሕ ንጽግ ክንጸግ ዝቃለስ ዓይነት።

(Sle'zi nibrettu akafalom. Kemamu teray. Sibket yelen. Qidme kunet yelen. Iti abo biqelilu yhib.)
Scene 6
[PICTURE: The younger son in a far-off city, surrounded by false friends at lavish parties. He throws gold coins in the air, drinks from expensive cups. The people around him have greedy, opportunistic eyes. Bright but hollow colors. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

"Not long after that, the younger son got together all he had, set off for a distant country and there squandered his wealth in wild living."

The far country isn't just a geographical location — it's a spiritual state. It's anywhere we go to escape the Father's presence. The son wanted the father's gifts without the father's company. He wanted blessing without relationship. Freedom without love.

And at first, it felt amazing. Money attracts friends. Wild living feels like freedom. Every party seemed to prove he'd made the right choice. But the "friends" who surrounded him didn't love him — they loved his money. And money always runs out.

"ድሕሪ ቍሩብ ግዜ እቲ ንእሽቶ ወዲ ኵሉ ዘለዎ ኣኻኺቡ ናብ ርሑቕ ሃገር ከደ ኣብኡ ድማ ንብረቱ ኣብ ዱር ናብራ ዘረገ።"

እታ ርሑቕ ሃገር ጂኦግራፍያዊ ቦታ ጥራይ ኣይኮነትን — መንፈሳዊ ኵነታት እያ። ካብ ህላወ ኣቦ ንምህዳም ዝኸድናዮ ዝኾነ ቦታ እያ። እቲ ወዲ ናይ ኣቦኡ ህያባት ብዘይ ናይ ኣቦኡ ኩባንያ ይደሊ ነበረ። ብዘይ ዝምድና በረኸት ይደሊ ነበረ። ብዘይ ፍቕሪ ናጽነት።

"After he had spent everything, there was a severe famine in that whole country, and he began to be in need."

Notice: it wasn't the famine alone that ruined him — it was that he'd already spent everything. When the hard times came, he had no reserves. His "friends" vanished like morning mist. Not one person offered him a meal, a room, or a job. The party was over, and he was utterly alone.

Sin always promises more than it delivers. It promises freedom but delivers slavery. It promises fulfillment but delivers emptiness. It promises community but delivers isolation. Every rebellion against God follows this exact pattern: excitement, emptiness, desperation.

ሓጢኣት ኩሉ ግዜ ካብ ዝህቦ ንላዕሊ ይተምን። ናጽነት ይተምን ግን ባርነት ይህብ። ምጽጋብ ይተምን ግን ባዶነት ይህብ። ሕብረት ይተምን ግን ምግላል ይህብ። ኵሉ ኣንጻር ኣምላኽ ዝግበር ዓመጽ ነዚ ልክዕ ቅዲ ይስዕብ፡ ህርፋን፡ ባዶነት፡ ስቓይ።

"So he went and hired himself out to a citizen of that country, who sent him to his fields to feed pigs. He longed to fill his stomach with the pods that the pigs were eating, but no one gave him anything."

For a Jewish young man, this was absolute rock bottom. Pigs were unclean animals — touching them made you ceremonially defiled. He wasn't just poor; he was religiously contaminated. He had fallen so far that he envied animals. He was hungrier than the pigs.

"No one gave him anything." This is the final verdict on the far country. All those people he'd partied with, all those "friends" he'd spent his money on — when he had nothing left to give, they had nothing left for him. The far country always takes everything and gives nothing back.

"When he came to himself, he said, 'How many of my father's hired servants have food to spare, and here I am starving to death!'"

"He came to himself." What a remarkable phrase. It means that all the time he was in the far country, spending and partying and sinking — he was not himself. Sin doesn't make you more free; it makes you less yourself. The far country promised him his true identity, but it stole it instead.

Coming to yourself means finally seeing clearly. It means the fog lifts. It means truth breaks through the lies you've been living. And the first thing the truth showed him was this: even his father's servants lived better than he did now. His father's house — the one he'd rejected — was full of abundance.

"ናብ ርእሱ ምስ መጸ በለ፡ 'ክንደይ ናይ ኣቦይ ተቖጻጸርቲ ዝተርፎም ምግቢ ኣለዎም ኣነ ግና ኣብዚ ብጥሜት ይመውት ኣለኹ!'"

ናብ ርእስኻ ምምጻእ ኣብ መወዳእታ ብንጹር ምርኣይ ማለት እዩ። ጋግ ይልዕል ማለት እዩ። ሓቂ ንኵሉ ዝነበርካዮ ሓሶት ይስበሮ ማለት እዩ። ሓቂ ዝመጀመርያ ዘርኣዮ ድማ እዚ ነበረ፡ ናይ ኣቦኡ ሰራሕተኛታት'ውን ካብኡ ዝሓሸ ይነብሩ ነበሩ። ገዛ ኣቦኡ — ዝነጸጎ — ብብዝሒ ዝመልአ ነበረ።

"I will set out and go back to my father and say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me like one of your hired servants."

Notice his plan: he would earn his way back. He would work off his debt. He would become a servant — not a son. He couldn't imagine that his father might simply forgive him and restore him. His speech was carefully rehearsed: confess, declare unworthiness, negotiate a demotion.

This is how we all think about God. We think we must clean ourselves up before coming home. We think we must earn our way back into favor. We think grace must be deserved. But if it must be deserved, it isn't grace — it's wages. And the father in this story doesn't deal in wages.

"ተንሲአ ናብ ኣቦይ ክኸይድ'የ ከምዚ ድማ ክብሎ'የ፡ ኣቦ ንሰማይን ንዓኻን ሓጢኣት ገይረ። ወድኻ ክብሃል ድሮ ዘይብቑዕ'የ፤ ከም ሓደ ተቖጻጻሪኻ ግበረኒ።"

ውጥኑ ኣስተብህሉ፡ ብስራሕ ክምለስ ይደሊ ነበረ። ዕዳኡ ክኸፍል ይደሊ ነበረ። ኣገልጋሊ ክኸውን ይደሊ ነበረ — ወዲ ዘይኮነ። ኣቦኡ ብቐሊሉ ይቕረ ኢሉ ክመልሶ ከም ዝኽእል ክሓስብ ኣይከኣለን። ዘረባኡ ብጥንቃቐ ዝተለማመዶ ነበረ፡ ምእማን፡ ዘይብቑዕነት ምእዋጅ፡ ምውራድ ምልዛብ።

ኵላትና ብዛዕባ ኣምላኽ ከምዚ ንሓስብ። ቅድሚ ምምላስ ርእስና ከነጽርይ ከም ዘለና ንሓስብ። ናብ ሞገስ ክንምለስ ብስራሕ ከም ዘለና ንሓስብ። ጸጋ ክብቅዕ ኣለዎ ንብል ንሓስብ። ክብቅዕ እንተ ኣለዎ ግን ጸጋ ኣይኮነን — ደመወዝ እዩ። እቲ ኣብ ዛንታ ዘሎ ኣቦ ግና ብደመወዝ ኣይኸይድን።

"But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him." Stop here. Read that again. While he was STILL a long way off.

How does a father see someone who is still far away? Only one way: he was watching. Every day. Scanning the horizon. Hoping. Waiting. Never giving up. The father didn't write off his son. He didn't say, "He made his bed, let him lie in it." He didn't move on with his life. He watched the road, day after day after day.

This is God. Before you even begin your journey home, He is already looking for you. Before your prayer of repentance leaves your lips, He has already seen you. The distance between you and God? He's been watching across it every single moment, waiting for the first sign of your return.

"ግን ገና ርሒቑ ከሎ ኣቦኡ ረኣዮ።" ኣብዚ ደው በሉ። ከም ብሓድሽ ኣንብብዎ። ገና ርሒቑ ከሎ።

ኣቦ ገና ርሒቑ ዘሎ ሰብ ብኸመይ ይርኢ? ብሓንቲ መንገዲ ጥራይ፡ ይርእይ ነበረ። ኵሉ መዓልቲ። ሰማይ ይምርምር ነበረ። ተስፋ ይገብር ነበረ። ይጽበ ነበረ። ፈጺሙ ኣይተቛርጸን። ኣቦ ንወዱ ኣይሓደጎን። "ከምቲ ዝዘረኦ ይኣክሎ" ኣይበለን። ህይወቱ ኣይቐጸለን። ነታ መንገዲ ይጥምታ ነበረ፡ መዓልቲ ብድሕሪ መዓልቲ ብድሕሪ መዓልቲ።

Scene 12
[PICTURE: The elderly father RUNNING down the road with his robes hitched up, sandals flying, dignified turban loosened — completely undignified. His face is pure joy, tears streaming down. "He was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him."

The father RUNS. In the ancient Middle East, a dignified older man never ran. Running meant hitching up your robes, exposing your legs — it was considered deeply shameful. Elders walked slowly. They waited for others to come to them. Running was for children and servants.

But this father doesn't care about his dignity. His love is more powerful than his pride. He runs because he cannot wait one more second. He runs because his son is alive. He runs because that's what love does — it doesn't stand on ceremony. It moves. It pursues. It crosses every distance.

He threw his arms around a son who smelled like pigs. He kissed a face that hadn't been washed in months. He embraced a boy who had wished him dead. This is the love of God — undignified, passionate, relentless, unconditional.

Grace interrupts our attempts to earn it. Every time we try to make deals with God — "I'll be better, I'll try harder, I'll do more" — grace says, "Stop. You're already mine."

Here is the revolutionary insight of this parable: there are TWO ways to be lost. Two ways to be separated from the father's love. Two ways to miss the party.

The YOUNGER son's way: open rebellion. Breaking the rules. Running away. Choosing sin over God. This is obvious lostness — everyone can see it, including the lost person themselves.

The OLDER son's way: moral conformity. Keeping the rules. Staying home. Choosing performance over relationship. This is hidden lostness — no one can see it, especially not the lost person themselves.

Both sons wanted the father's things without the father himself. The younger one took them and left. The older one stayed to earn them. But neither one wanted a relationship — they wanted a transaction. And both ended up outside the party, separated from the father's joy.

So who is the real "prodigal" in this story? The word "prodigal" doesn't mean "lost" or "rebellious." It means RECKLESSLY EXTRAVAGANT — spending without limit, giving without calculation.

The younger son was prodigal with his father's money. But the FATHER is prodigal with his love. He gives freedom recklessly. He waits faithfully regardless of cost. He runs shamelessly. He forgives instantly. He restores completely. He celebrates extravagantly. He pursues relentlessly — both the rebel and the religious.

The real prodigal is GOD. His love is reckless. His grace is extravagant. His mercy is limitless. His pursuit is relentless. He is the prodigal God — the God who spends His love without calculation, without condition, without limit. He is not a careful, measured God. He is a reckless, passionate, overwhelming Father who will stop at nothing to bring His children home.

So — which son are you? Be honest. Most of us are a mix of both.

Maybe you're the younger son — running from God, chasing things that promise freedom but deliver emptiness. Maybe you're in the far country right now, feeding pigs, wondering how you got here. The father is watching the road for you. He's ready to run.

Or maybe you're the older son — doing all the "right" things but with a cold heart. Going to church, reading your Bible, following the rules — but not out of love. Out of duty. Out of fear. Out of a desire to earn what's already freely given. You're in the father's house, but you've never experienced the father's joy.

The Moral of the Story

English: God is the Prodigal Father — recklessly extravagant in His love for both the rebellious and the self-righteous. There are two ways to be lost: running from God through sin, or staying near God through performance without love. Both miss the Father's heart. But the Father pursues both sons with the same relentless, undignified, overwhelming love. Grace cannot be earned — it can only be received. The party is waiting. Come in.

1. ኣየናይ ወዲ ዝያዳ ይመሳሰለካ — ብግሊጽ ዝዓሙጽ ንእሽቶ ወዲ ወይ ብደገ ዝእዘዝ ግን ብውሽጢ ዝቕየም ዓቢ ወዲ? ቅኑዕ ኩን። ኣብ ኣየናይ ክፋል ህይወትካ ከም ኣየናይ ወዲ ትሰርሕ ትርኢ? ምስ ኣምላኽ ዝምድና ብዘይ ምድላይ ናይ ኣምላኽ በረኸት ዝደለኻሉ ግዜ ከተለሊ ትኽእል'ዶ?

2. Do you think the older brother is actually MORE lost than the younger brother? Why or why not? The younger son knew he was lost and came home. The older son didn't even realize he was lost. Which is more dangerous — obvious sin or hidden self-righteousness?

2. ዓቢ ሓው ካብ ንእሽቶ ሓው ብሓቂ ዝያዳ ጠፊኡ ኢልካ ትሓስብ'ዶ? ንምንታይ ወይ ንምንታይ ኣይኮነ? ንእሽቶ ወዲ ጠፊኡ ከም ዘሎ ፈሊጡ ተመሊሱ። ዓቢ ወዲ ጠፊኡ ከም ዘሎ'ውን ኣይፈለጠን። ኣየናይ ዝያዳ ሓደገኛ'ዩ — ግሉጽ ሓጢኣት ወይ ሕቡእ ርእሰ ጽድቂ?

3. Why is grace so offensive to religious people? Why would the older brother be angry at his father's generosity? Have you ever felt angry or resentful when God seemed to bless someone you thought didn't deserve it? What does that reaction reveal about your own heart?

3. ንምንታይ ጸጋ ንሃይማኖተኛታት ኣዝዩ ዘቖጥዕ'ዩ? ንምንታይ ዓቢ ሓው ብልግሲ ኣቦኡ ይቖጥዕ? ኣምላኽ ዘይብቑዕ ኢልካ ዝሓሰብካ ሰብ ዝባረኽ ምስ ዝመስለካ ተቖጢዕካ ወይ ተቐይምካ ትፈልጥ'ዶ? ቲ ግብረ መልሲ ብዛዕባ ናይ ገዛእ ርእስኻ ልቢ እንታይ ይቃልዕ?

4. How does the father in this story represent God? List every action the father takes and explain what it reveals about God's character. Why does Jesus emphasize that the father RUNS? What does it mean that God is not a careful, distant God but a passionate, pursuing Father?

4. ኣብ ዛንታ ዘሎ ኣቦ ብኸመይ ንኣምላኽ ይውክል? ኣቦ ዝወስዶ ኵሉ ስጉምቲ ዘርዚርካ ብዛዕባ ባህሪ ኣምላኽ እንታይ ከም ዝቃልዕ ግለጽ። የሱስ ኣቦ ከም ዝጐይይ ንምንታይ ኣጽዒቑ ይገልጽ? ኣምላኽ ጥንቁቕ ርሑቕ ኣምላኽ ዘይኮነ ህሩፍ ዝደሊ ኣቦ ምዃኑ እንታይ ማለት'ዩ?

5. What does it mean that God "runs" toward us? If God runs toward both the rebellious and the self-righteous, what should our response be? How would your daily life change if you truly believed that God is not keeping score — that He's not a demanding employer but a loving Father who already calls you "son" or "daughter"?

5. ኣምላኽ ናባና "ይጐይይ" ማለት እንታይ ማለት'ዩ? ኣምላኽ ናብ ዓመጸኛታትን ርእሰ ጽድቂ ዘለዎምን ማዕረ ዝጐይይ እንተ ኮይኑ ግብረ መልስና እንታይ ክኸውን ኣለዎ? ኣምላኽ ሒሳብ ከም ዘይቈጽር — ዝሓትት ወሃብ ስራሕ ዘይኮነ ድሮ "ወደይ" ወይ "ጓለይ" ዝብለካ ፈቃር ኣቦ ምዃኑ ብሓቂ እንተ ኣሚንካ ዕለታዊ ህይወትካ ብኸመይ ይቕየር ነበረ?

A Prayer of Gratitude for the Prodigal Father

Father God — our Prodigal Father — we come to You amazed by what we've seen today. You are not the distant, stern, score-keeping God we imagined. You are the Father who runs. You are the God who leaves the party to find the one standing alone outside. You are love without limit, grace without condition, mercy without end.

We confess that we are both sons. We have run from You into far countries of our own making — chasing pleasures that promised everything and delivered emptiness. And we have stayed in Your house with cold hearts — doing the "right things" for the wrong reasons, serving You like slaves instead of living like beloved children.

Forgive us for wanting Your blessings without wanting You. Forgive us for treating Your grace as something to earn rather than something to receive. Forgive us for resenting the grace You show to others. Forgive us for keeping score in a kingdom built on gift.

Thank You for being the Prodigal God — recklessly generous with Your love. Thank You that You saw us when we were still a long way off. Thank You that You ran toward us before we could finish our rehearsed speeches. Thank You for the robe of righteousness, the ring of authority, and the sandals of belonging that You place on us — not because we earned them, but because we are Yours.

Help us to come into the party. Help us to stop standing outside — whether in rebellion or in religion — and simply come in and celebrate with You. Teach us to receive what we cannot earn. Teach us to rest in what we cannot achieve. Teach us to be sons and daughters, not servants and slaves.

And Father, for those of us who are still in the far country — give us the courage to come to ourselves and turn our faces toward home. You are watching the road. You are ready to run. We are coming home. In the name of Jesus, the One who told this story to show us Your heart. Amen.

ኣቦ ኣምላኽ — ፕሮዲጋል ኣቦና — ሎሚ ብዝረኣናዮ ተገሪምና ናባኻ ንመጽእ። ርሑቕ ጽኑዕ ሒሳብ ዝቈጽር ኣምላኽ ኣይኮንካን ከም ዝሓሰብናዮ። ዝጐይይ ኣቦ ኢኻ። ንበይኑ ኣብ ደገ ንዝቐወመ ንምርካብ ነቲ በዓል ገዲፉ ዝወጽእ ኣምላኽ ኢኻ። ደረት ኣልቦ ፍቕሪ ቅድመ ኵነት ኣልቦ ጸጋ መወዳእታ ኣልቦ ምሕረት ኢኻ።

ክልቲኦም ኣወዳት ምዃንና ንእመን። ናብ ናይ ገዛእ ርእስና ርሑቕ ሃገራት ሃዲምና — ኵሉ ዝተመን ግን ባዶነት ዝሃበ ሓጐስ ሰዒብና። ብዝሑቑ ልቢ ድማ ኣብ ገዝኣኻ ጸኒሕና — "ቅኑዕ ነገር" ብጌጋ ምኽንያት ንገብር ከም ባሮት ንግልገለካ ኣብ ክንዲ ከም ፍቑራት ደቂ ንነብር።

ንዓኻ ብዘይ ምድላይ በረኸትካ ስለ ዝደለና ይቕረ በለና። ጸጋኻ ክምጻእ ዘለዎ ብምቕባል ዘይኮነ ንኣትሓሳስባና ይቕረ በለና። ንኻልኦት ዘርኣኻዮ ጸጋ ስለ ዝቐየምና ይቕረ በለና። ኣብ ህያብ ዝተሃንጸ መንግስቲ ሒሳብ ስለ ዝቈጸርና ይቕረ በለና።

ፕሮዲጋል ኣምላኽ ስለ ዝኾንካ — ብፍቕርኻ ብዘይ ልክዕ ልግሲ — ነመስግነካ። ገና ርሒቕና ከለና ስለ ዝረኣኻና ነመስግነካ። ዝተለማመድናዮ ዘረባ ከይወዳእና ናባና ስለ ዝጐየኻ ነመስግነካ። ናይ ጽድቂ ልብሲ ናይ ስልጣን ቀለቤት ናይ ምብጻሕ ሳእኒ ኣብ ልዕሌና ስለ ዘንበርካ ነመስግነካ — ስለ ዝተመሃርናዮ ዘይኮነ ናትካ ስለ ዝኾንና።

ናብ ቲ በዓል ክንኣቱ ሓግዘና። ኣብ ደገ ደው ምባል ከንውድእ — ብዓመጽ ይኹን ብሃይማኖት — ብቐሊሉ ኣቲና ምሳኻ ከነብዕል ሓግዘና። ክንምሃሮ ዘይንኽእል ክንቅበል ምሃረና። ክንወቅዖ ዘይንኽእል ኣብኡ ክንዓርፍ ምሃረና። ኣወዳትን ኣዋልድን ክንከውን ምሃረና ኣገልገልትን ባሮትን ዘይኮነ።

ኣቦ ገና ኣብ ርሑቕ ሃገር ንዘለና — ናብ ርእስና ንምምጻእ ገጽና ናብ ገዛ ንምቕናዕ ትብዓት ሃበና። ነታ መንገዲ ትጥምታ ኣለኻ። ክትጐይይ ድሉው ኢኻ። ናብ ገዛ ንመጽእ ኣለና። ልብኻ ንምርኣይ ነዛ ዛንታ ዝነገረ ብስም የሱስ። ኣሜን።
