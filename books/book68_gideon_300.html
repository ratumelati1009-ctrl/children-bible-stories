For seven long years, the people of Israel lived in terror. Every harvest season, the Midianites swept in like locusts — thousands upon thousands of warriors on camels, destroying every crop, stealing every animal, leaving nothing alive. The Israelites abandoned their homes and fled to caves in the mountains, to crevices in the rocks, to any hole they could hide in. Imagine being so afraid that you cannot live in your own house. Imagine planting seeds knowing someone will likely steal the harvest. Imagine your children going hungry because an enemy takes everything you grow. This was Israel's reality — not for a week or a month, but for seven years. The nation that God had called out of Egypt, the people He had given the Promised Land, were now hiding underground like animals. And the worst part? They had brought this on themselves. They had turned away from God, worshipped the Baals, and now they were experiencing life without His protection.

Finally, after seven years of suffering, Israel cried out to God. This is the pattern that repeats throughout the book of Judges — rebellion, ruin, repentance, rescue. The people turn from God, disaster follows, they cry out in desperation, and God sends a deliverer. It happened with Othniel, with Ehud, with Deborah, and now it would happen again. But notice something important: God did not rescue them immediately. First, He sent a prophet who reminded them why they were suffering. "I brought you out of Egypt," God said through the prophet. "I gave you this land. I said do not worship the gods of the Amorites. But you did not listen to me." God wanted them to understand the connection between their choices and their consequences. He was not being cruel by waiting — He was being thorough. A rescue without understanding leads to the same cycle repeating. God wanted more than their relief; He wanted their hearts. And sometimes, understanding comes only through suffering.

Then the Angel of the Lord appeared — not in a temple, not on a mountain, not to a priest or a warrior. He appeared to a young man named Gideon who was threshing wheat in a winepress. Think about that. Threshing wheat is normally done on a hilltop where the wind can blow away the chaff. But Gideon was doing it in a winepress — a pit dug into the ground — because he was hiding from the Midianites. He was doing essential work in the worst possible way because fear controlled his life. And in this moment of fear and hiddenness, the Angel says the most unexpected thing: "The Lord is with you, mighty warrior!" Mighty warrior? This man was hiding in a hole! He was the picture of defeat, not victory. He was cowering, not conquering. Yet God saw something in Gideon that Gideon could not see in himself. God was not describing Gideon's present — He was declaring Gideon's future. God calls things that are not as though they were.

Gideon's response is one of the most honest in all of Scripture. He does not say "Yes, Lord, I'm ready!" He does not stand tall with sudden confidence. Instead, he argues. "Pardon me, my lord, but if the Lord is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all His wonders that our ancestors told us about?" Gideon had real questions — and God did not rebuke him for asking them. Then Gideon makes his deepest objection: "But Lord, how can I save Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my family." Do you hear the layers of inadequacy? Not just "I'm weak," but "my whole family is weak, my whole clan is weak, and even among the weak ones, I'm the weakest." Gideon was at the very bottom of every category that mattered. He had no army, no reputation, no resources, no experience. He was a nobody from a family of nobodies. And THIS is exactly who God chose.

God's answer to Gideon's inadequacy is stunning in its simplicity. He does not argue with Gideon's assessment. He does not say "You're stronger than you think" or "Believe in yourself." He does not list Gideon's hidden talents or untapped potential. Instead, God simply says: "I will be with you, and you will strike down all the Midianites, leaving none alive." Do you see what God did? He did not deny Gideon's weakness. He did not pretend Gideon was adequate. Instead, He made Gideon's adequacy irrelevant by promising His own presence. The math changed entirely. It was no longer "Gideon versus the Midianites." It was now "God, who happens to be with Gideon, versus the Midianites." And that changes everything. This is God's consistent answer throughout all of Scripture: not "you can do it" but "I will be with you." He said it to Moses, to Joshua, to Jeremiah, to Mary, to the disciples. Your weakness is not the issue. His presence is the answer.

It is something to offer to God. "Here, Lord — I am not enough. I am the least. I have nothing impressive to bring. But You said that is exactly where Your power shines brightest." Paul's response to this truth was radical: "Therefore I will boast all the more gladly about my weaknesses, so that Christ's power may rest on me." He went from begging God to remove his weakness to boasting about it. Because he understood: weakness surrendered to God becomes the stage for God's greatest performances. You are not too weak for God. You might be weak enough.
ሃዋርያ ጳውሎስ ሰለስተ ግዜ ኣምላኽ ድኽመቱ ከልግስ ጸለየ። ሰለስተ ግዜ ኣምላኽ ኣይፋል በለ። ድሕሪኡ ኣምላኽ ምኽንያት ሃበ፡ "ጸጋይ ይኣኽለካ ምኽንያቱ ሓይለይ ኣብ ድኽመት ይፍጸም" (2ይ ቆሮንጦስ 12:9)። ናይ ኣምላኽ ሓይሊ ኣብ ሓይልኻ ኣይፍጸምን። ኣብ ክእለትካ ኣይኮነን። ኣብ ርእሰ ተኣማንነትካ ኣይኮነን። ኣብ ጸጋታትካ ኣይኮነን። ሓይሉ ኣብ ድኽመትካ ይፍጸም — ይውዳእ፡ ብምሉኡ ይርአ፡ ናብ ዝለዓለ መግለጺ ይመጽእ። እዚ ማለት ድኽመትካ ዘሕፍር ኣይኮነን። ንኣምላኽ ከተቕርብ ትኽእል ነገር እዩ። "ኣነ ኣኻሊ ኣይኮንኩን ጐይታ። ኣነ ዝነኣሰ እየ። ዘገርም ዘቕርብ የብለይን። ግናኸ ሓይልኻ ዝደመቐ ዝብርህ ልክዕ ኣብኡ ምዃኑ ኢልካ ኣለኻ።" ጳውሎስ ነዚ ሓቂ ዝሃቦ መልሲ ሰብኣዊ ነበረ፡ "ስለዚ ብድኽመታተይ ብዝያዳ ብሓጐስ ክምካሕ እየ ሓይሊ ክርስቶስ ኣብ ልዕለይ ክዓርፍ።" ኣምላኽ ድኽመቱ ከልግስ ካብ ምልማን ብኡ ምምካሕ ከደ። ተረድአ ድማ፡ ንኣምላኽ ዝተሓደገ ድኽመት ንዝዓበየ ስራሓት ኣምላኽ መድረኽ ይኸውን። ንኣምላኽ ብዙሕ ድኹም ኣይኮንካን። ኣኻሊ ድኹም ክትከውን ትኽእል።

The Moral of the Story / ትምህርቲ ናይ ታሪኽ

Key Verse: "You have too many men. I cannot deliver Midian into their hands, or Israel would boast against me, saying, 'My own strength has saved me.'" — Judges 7:2

ቀንዲ ጥቕሲ: "ብዙሓት ሰባት ኣለዉኻ። ንሚድያን ኣብ ኢዶም ከሕልፎ ኣይክእልን ወይ እስራኤል ኣንጻረይ ክምካሕ 'ናተይ ሓይሊ ኣድሒኑኒ' ክብል።" — መሳፍንቲ 7:2

God does not call the qualified — He qualifies the called. He does not need your strength, your talent, your numbers, or your resources. He needs your availability and your willingness to be broken so His light can shine through. Your weakness is not your disqualification — it is your qualification. When you feel like you are not enough, you are finally in the position where God can use you most powerfully. Because it was never about you being enough. It was always about Him being more than enough through you.

ኣምላኽ ንብቑዓት ኣይጽውዕን — ንዝጸውዖም የብቅዕ። ሓይልኻ፡ ክእለትካ፡ ቁጽርኻ ወይ ጸጋታትካ ኣየድልዮን። ምህላውካን ብርሃኑ ክበርህ ብርሃኑ ክትስበር ፍቓደኛ ምዃንካን ይደሊ። ድኽመትካ ብቑዕ ዘይምዃንካ ኣይኮነትን — ብቑዕ ምዃንካ እያ። ኣኻሊ ከም ዘይኮንካ ምስ ትስመዓካ ኣብ መወዳእታ ኣምላኽ ብዝበለጸ ክጥቀመልካ ዝኽእል ቦታ ኣለኻ። ምኽንያቱ ፈጺሙ ብዛዕባ ንስኻ ኣኻሊ ምዃንካ ኣይነበረን። ኩሉ ግዜ ብዛዕባ ንሱ ብኣኻ ኣቢሉ ካብ ዝኣኽል ንላዕሊ ምዃኑ ነበረ።

Discussion Questions / ሕቶታት ምይይጥ

  1. Have you ever felt like the least qualified person for something God might be calling you to do? How does Gideon's story change the way you see your inadequacy?
    ኣምላኽ ክጽውዓካ ንዝኽእል ነገር ዝነኣሰ ብቑዕ ሰብ ኮንካ ተሰሚዑካ ይፈልጥ? ናይ ጊድኦን ታሪኽ ንዘይምእኩልነትካ ብኸመይ ይቕይሮ?
  2. Why do you think God uses weakness instead of strength? What would happen if God only used impressive, talented, confident people?
    ኣምላኽ ኣብ ክንዲ ሓይሊ ድኽመት ስለምንታይ ይጥቀም ትሓስብ? ኣምላኽ ዘገርሙ፡ ክእለት ዘለዎም፡ ርእሰ ተኣማንነት ዘለዎም ሰባት ጥራይ ዝጥቀም ነይሩ እንተዝኸውን እንታይ ምኾነ?
  3. Gideon asked for signs (the fleece) because he was afraid. Is it okay to ask God for confirmation when you're scared? Where is the line between honest doubt and demanding signs?
    ጊድኦን ስለ ዝፈርሐ ምልክታት (ፈሰስ) ሓተተ። ምስ ተፈራሕካ ኣምላኽ ምርግጋጽ ምሕታት ጽቡቕ ድዩ? ኣብ መንጎ ቅኑዕ ጥርጣረን ምልክት ምሕታትን ሕድ ኣበይ ኣሎ?
  4. God used unconventional methods (torches, jars, trumpets instead of swords). Can you think of a time God worked in your life through unexpected or unusual means?
    ኣምላኽ ዘይልሙድ ኣገባባት ተጠቕመ (ኣብ ክንዲ ሰይፊ ሽግ፡ ዕትሮ፡ መለኸት)። ኣምላኽ ኣብ ህይወትካ ብዘይትጽበዮ ወይ ዘይልሙድ ኣገባብ ዝሰርሐ ግዜ ክትሓስብ ትኽእል?
  5. The jars had to be broken for the light to shine. What does this teach about suffering, failure, or brokenness in our lives? How might your "broken places" be exactly where God's light shines brightest?
    ብርሃን ክበርህ ዕትሮ ክስበር ነበሮ። እዚ ብዛዕባ ስቓይ፡ ውድቀት ወይ ስብራት ኣብ ህይወትና እንታይ ይምህር? "ዝተሰበረ ቦታታትካ" ብርሃን ኣምላኽ ዝደመቐ ዝበርህ ልክዕ ቦታ ብኸመይ ክኸውን ይኽእል?

Let Us Pray / ንጸሊ

English:

Dear God, thank You that You do not need me to be strong. Thank You that my weakness is not a disqualification but an invitation for Your power. I confess that I have been afraid — afraid that I am not enough, afraid that my smallness disqualifies me, afraid that You could never use someone like me. But today I choose to believe what You told Gideon: "I will be with you." Lord, take my weakness. Take my inadequacy. Take my fear and my doubt. Use them. Break my jar of clay so Your light can shine through me. I do not need to be impressive — I just need to be available. I do not need to be strong — I just need to be Yours. Let my life prove that Your power is made perfect in weakness. Let people see You, not me, when they look at what You accomplish through my surrendered life. I am Your 300, Lord. Use me for Your glory. Amen.

ትግርኛ:

ዝኸበርካ ኣምላኽ፡ ሓያል ክኸውን ከም ዘየድልየካ ነመስግነካ። ድኽመተይ ብቑዕ ዘይምዃን ዘይኮነ ንሓይልኻ ዕድመ ምዃኑ ነመስግነካ። ፈሪሐ ከም ዝጸናሕኩ ይእመን — ኣኻሊ ከም ዘይኮንኩ ፈሪሐ፡ ንእስነተይ ብቑዕ ዘይምዃን ከም ዝገብረኒ ፈሪሐ፡ ከማይ ዝኣመሰለ ፈጺምካ ክትጥቀም ከም ዘይትኽእል ፈሪሐ። ግናኸ ሎሚ ንጊድኦን ዝበልካዮ ክኣምን ይመርጽ፡ "ኣነ ምሳኻ ክኸውን እየ።" ጐይታ ድኽመተይ ውሰዶ። ዘይምእኩልነተይ ውሰዶ። ፍርሐይን ጥርጣረይን ውሰዶ። ተጠቐመሎም። ብርሃንካ ብኣይ ክበርህ ናይ ሸክላ ዕትሮይ ስበሮ። ዘገርም ክኸውን ኣየድልየንን — ድሉው ክኸውን ጥራይ ይደሊ። ሓያል ክኸውን ኣየድልየንን — ናትካ ክኸውን ጥራይ ይደሊ። ህይወተይ ሓይልኻ ኣብ ድኽመት ከም ዝፍጸም ተረጋግጽ። ሰባት ብዝተሓደገ ህይወተይ ኣቢልካ ንዝተፈጸመ ክርእዩ ከለዉ ንዓይ ዘይኮነ ንዓኻ ይርኣዩ። ናይ 300 ናትካ እየ ጐይታ። ንኽብርኻ ተጠቐመለይ። ኣሜን።
