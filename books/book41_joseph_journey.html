Joseph's Journey: From Slavery to Power

ጉዕዞ ዮሴፍ፡ ካብ ባርነት ናብ ስልጣን

Written by Daniel Tesfamariam

Ages 10-17

Little Believers Tigrinya Bible Stories
Book 41 of 60

Scene 1 of 25
[PICTURE: Jacob lovingly placing a magnificent multi-colored coat on young Joseph's shoulders, while his older brothers watch from a distance with resentful expressions. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Jacob had twelve sons, but Joseph held a special place in his heart. He was the firstborn of Rachel, the wife Jacob had loved most deeply. To show his favor, Jacob gave Joseph a richly ornamented coat — a garment of many colors that set him apart from his brothers. This visible display of favoritism planted seeds of bitterness that would soon grow into something dangerous.
ያዕቆብ ዓሰርተ ክልተ ደቂ ነበርዎ፡ ግን ዮሴፍ ፍሉይ ቦታ ኣብ ልቡ ነበሮ። ንሱ በኹሪ ራሄል፡ እታ ያዕቆብ ኣዝዩ ዘፍቅራ ሰበይቱ ነበረ። ፍቕሩ ንምርኣይ፡ ያዕቆብ ንዮሴፍ ብዙሕ ሕብሪ ዘለዎ ክቡር ቀሚሽ ሃቦ — ካብ ኣሕዋቱ ዝፈልዮ ክዳን። እዚ ግሉጽ ኣድልዎ ዘርኢ ምርግጋጽ ናይ ምረት ዘርኢ ዘራእቲ ኣብ ሓደ ሓደገኛ ነገር ክዓቢ ዝኽእል ተኸለ።
(Ya'iqob aserte kilte deqi neberwo, gin Yosef filuy bota ab libu nebero.)
Scene 2 of 25
[PICTURE: Young Joseph excitedly telling his brothers about his dream, with sheaves of wheat bowing down to one standing sheaf. Brothers looking angry and dismissive. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Joseph had vivid dreams that seemed to carry divine significance. In one dream, he and his brothers were binding sheaves of grain in the field when suddenly his sheaf rose and stood upright, while their sheaves gathered around and bowed down to his. When Joseph naively shared this dream, his brothers' jealousy deepened into hatred. "Do you intend to reign over us?" they demanded. Joseph did not yet understand that speaking God's truth requires wisdom in timing.
ዮሴፍ ኣምላኻዊ ትርጉም ዝመስል ንጹር ሕልምታት ነበሮ። ኣብ ሓደ ሕልሚ፡ ንሱን ኣሕዋቱን ኣብ ግራት ኣእዳው ይኣስሩ ነበሩ፡ ሃንደበት ናቱ ኣእዳው ተንሲኡ ደው ኢሉ፡ ናቶም ኣእዳው ድማ ኣብ ዙርያኡ ተኣኪቦም ሰገዱሉ። ዮሴፍ ብንጹህነት እዚ ሕልሚ ምስ ኣካፈሎም፡ ቅንኢ ኣሕዋቱ ናብ ጽልኢ ዓበየ። "ክትነግሰልናዶ ኢኻ ትሓስብ?" ኢሎም ሓተትዎ። ዮሴፍ ገና ሓቂ ኣምላኽ ምዝራብ ናይ ግዜ ጥበብ ከም ዝሓትት ኣይተረድኦን።
(Yosef amlakhawi tirgum zimesil nitsur hilmitat nebero.)
Scene 3 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph having a second dream - the sun, moon, and eleven stars bowing before him, depicted in a night sky vision above his sleeping form. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
A second dream followed — even more provocative than the first. Joseph saw the sun, moon, and eleven stars all bowing down to him. Even his father Jacob rebuked him this time: "Shall your mother and I and your brothers actually come to bow down before you?" Yet Jacob quietly kept the matter in his heart, sensing perhaps that God was speaking through his young son. Sometimes divine purpose is revealed long before it is fulfilled, and patience is required of both the dreamer and those who hear.
ካልኣይ ሕልሚ ሰዓበ — ካብ ቀዳማይ ዝያዳ ብዱህ። ዮሴፍ ጸሓይ፡ ወርሒን ዓሰርተ ሓደ ከዋኽብትን ክሰግዱሉ ረኣየ። ኣቡኡ ያዕቆብ እውን ሕጂ ገሰጾ፡ "ኣነን ኣደኻን ኣሕዋትካን ብሓቂ ክንሰግደልካዶ ክንመጽእ ኢና?" ግን ያዕቆብ ብሕት ኢሉ ነቲ ነገር ኣብ ልቡ ሓዘዎ፡ ምናልባት ኣምላኽ ብመንእሰይ ወዱ ይዛረብ ከም ዘሎ ስለ ዝተሰመዖ። ሓደ ሓደ ግዜ ዕላማ ኣምላኽ ቅድሚ ምፍጻሙ ብዙሕ ቅድሚ ይገለጽ፡ ትዕግስቲ ድማ ካብ ሓላሚ ኮነ ካብ ሰማዒ ይሕተት።
(Kal'ay hilmi se'abe — kab qedamay ziyada biduh.)
Scene 4 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph walking alone across an open field toward his brothers who are gathered with their flocks in the distance at Dothan, unaware of the danger ahead. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
One day, Jacob sent Joseph to check on his brothers who were grazing their flocks far from home near Shechem. Joseph traveled alone, obedient to his father's request, not knowing he was walking into a trap. When his brothers saw him approaching from a distance — recognizable by that distinctive coat — dark resentment boiled over. "Here comes the dreamer," they sneered. In that moment, they began plotting to end both Joseph and his dreams permanently.
ሓደ መዓልቲ ያዕቆብ ንዮሴፍ ኣሕዋቱ ኣብ ጥቓ ሸኬም መጓሰ ክጓስዩ ከለዉ ክፍትሾም ለኣኸ። ዮሴፍ በይኑ ተጓዕዘ፡ ንትእዛዝ ኣቡኡ ተኣዛዚ፡ ናብ መጻወድያ ይኸይድ ከም ዘሎ ኣይፈለጠን። ኣሕዋቱ ካብ ርሑቕ ክመጽእ ምስ ረኣይዎ — ብፍሉይ ቀሚሹ ዝፍለጥ — ጸልማት ቅሬታ ፈሰሰ። "እንሆ ሓላሚ ይመጽእ ኣሎ" ኢሎም ኣላገጹ። ኣብቲ ህሞት፡ ንዮሴፍን ሕልምታቱን ንሓዋሩ ከጥፍኡ ክሓስቡ ጀመሩ።
(Hade me'alti Ya'iqob ni'Yosef ahwatu ab tsiqa Shekem megwase kigwasyu kelewo kifitishom le'akhe.)
Scene 5 of 25
[PICTURE: Brothers stripping the colorful coat from Joseph and throwing him into a deep, dry cistern. Joseph reaching up helplessly from the dark pit. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
They seized Joseph, stripped him of his beloved coat, and threw him into an empty cistern — a deep pit with no water. Reuben, the eldest, had secretly planned to rescue him later, but the others felt no such mercy. As Joseph cried out from the darkness of that pit, his brothers sat down to eat their meal, unmoved by his pleas. How quickly jealousy can harden the human heart, turning brothers into enemies and love into cruelty.
ንዮሴፍ ሒዞም፡ ፍቑር ቀሚሹ ቀንጢጦም፡ ናብ ጥራይ ጕድጓድ ደርበይዎ — ማይ ዘይብሉ ዓሚቝ ጒድጓድ። ሮቤል እቲ በኹሪ ብሕቡእ ድሒሩ ከድሕኖ ሓሲቡ ነበረ፡ ግን እቶም ካልኦት ከምዚ ዓይነት ምሕረት ኣይነበሮምን። ዮሴፍ ካብ ጸልማት ናይቲ ጕድጓድ ክጠርዕ ከሎ፡ ኣሕዋቱ ተቐሚጦም ምግቦም በልዑ፡ ብልመናኡ ዘይተነቓነቑ። ቅንኢ ክሳብ ክንደይ ቀልጢፉ ንልቢ ሰብ ከም ዘትርር፡ ኣሕዋት ናብ ጸላእቲ ፍቕሪ ድማ ናብ ጭካነ ከም ዝቕይር ሕሰብ።
(Ni'Yosef hizom, fiqur qemishu qentitom, nab tiray gudguad derbeywo.)
Scene 6 of 25
[PICTURE: A caravan of Ishmaelite merchants with camels approaching, while brothers negotiate to sell Joseph. Joseph being pulled from the pit looking terrified. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
As they ate, a caravan of Ishmaelite merchants appeared on the horizon, their camels laden with spices bound for Egypt. Judah saw an opportunity: "What profit is it if we kill our brother? Let us sell him instead." For twenty pieces of silver — the price of a young slave — they sold their own flesh and blood. Joseph, barely seventeen years old, was torn from everything he knew and carried away toward a foreign land. The brothers watched the caravan disappear, not realizing they had just set in motion God's sovereign plan for the salvation of nations.
ኣብ ዝበልዑሉ ግዜ፡ ናይ ኣስማኤላውያን ነጋዶ ንግደት ኣብ ባይታ ተራእየ፡ ኣግማሎም ብቐመማት ንግብጺ ዝተጻዕኑ። ይሁዳ ዕድል ረኣየ፡ "ንሓውና እንተ ቐቲልና እንታይ ረብሓ ኣለና? ኣብ ክንዳኡ ንሸጦ።" ብዕስራ ብሩር — ዋጋ ናይ መንእሰይ ባርያ — ናይ ገዛእ ርእሶም ስጋን ደምን ሸጡ። ዮሴፍ ገና ዓሰርተ ሸውዓተ ዓመቱ ከሎ ካብ ኩሉ ዝፈልጦ ተነጺሉ ናብ ወጻኢ ሃገር ተወስደ። ኣሕዋቱ ነቲ ንግደት ክጠፍእ ተዓዘቡ፡ ንድሕነት ሃገራት ዝኸውን ልዑል ዕላማ ኣምላኽ ኣብ ምንቅስቓስ ከም ዘእተዉ ዘይፈለጡ።
(Ab zibelullu gize, nay Asma'elawyan negado nigdet ab bayta tera'aye.)
Scene 7 of 25
[PICTURE: Brothers dipping Joseph's colorful coat in goat's blood, then presenting it to their grieving father Jacob who tears his garments in anguish. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
To conceal their crime, the brothers slaughtered a goat and dipped Joseph's coat in its blood. They brought it to their father and said, "We found this. Examine it — is it your son's coat?" Jacob recognized it immediately and was shattered with grief. "A wild animal has devoured him!" he wailed. He tore his clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourned for many days. No one could comfort him. The brothers lived with their lie, watching their father suffer — a reminder that sin always produces consequences beyond what we imagine.
ገበኖም ንምሽፋን፡ ኣሕዋት ጤል ሓሪዶም ቀሚሽ ዮሴፍ ኣብ ደሙ ጠመቕዎ። ናብ ኣቡኦም ኣምጺኦም "ነዚ ረኺብናዮ። መርምሮ — ናይ ወድኻ ቀሚሽ ድዩ?" በልዎ። ያዕቆብ ቀልጢፉ ለለዮ ብሓዘን ድማ ተሰበረ። "እንስሳ በረኻ በሊዕዎ!" ኢሉ ኣውየ። ክዳኑ ቀዲዱ ሸኽ ለቢሱ ብዙሕ መዓልቲ ሓዘነ። ዝኾነ ሰብ ከጸናንዖ ኣይከኣለን። ኣሕዋት ምስ ሓሶቶም ተቐሚጦም ኣቡኦም ክሳቐ ይዕዘቡ ነበሩ — ሓጢኣት ኩሉ ግዜ ካብ ዝሓሰብናዮ ንላዕሊ ሳዕቤናት ከም ዘፍሪ ዘዘክር።
(Gebenon limshifan, ahwat tel haridoom qemish Yosef ab demu temeqwo.)
Scene 8 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph being sold at a slave market in Egypt, with Potiphar - a tall Egyptian official - purchasing him. Egyptian architecture and palm trees in the background. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
In Egypt, Joseph was sold to Potiphar, the captain of Pharaoh's guard — a powerful and wealthy official. Stripped of his identity, his family, and his freedom, Joseph faced a choice that would define his character: would he become bitter, or would he remain faithful? Despite every reason to despair, Joseph chose to serve with excellence. He decided that even as a slave, he would honor God through his work. This decision — made in the depths of suffering — would prove to be the foundation of everything God would build through him.
ኣብ ግብጺ ዮሴፍ ንጶጢፋር፡ ሓለቓ ሓለዋ ፈርኦን — ሓያልን ሃብታምን ሰብ ስልጣን — ተሸጠ። ካብ መንነቱ፡ ስድራቤቱ፡ ናጽነቱ ተቐንጢጡ፡ ዮሴፍ ባህሪኡ ዝውስን ምርጫ ገጠሞ፡ ክምረር ድዩ ወይስ እሙን ክቕጽል? ንጓሂ ዘፍቅድ ኩሉ ምኽንያት እናሃለወ፡ ዮሴፍ ብብሉጽነት ከገልግል መረጸ። ከም ባርያ'ውን እንተኾነ ብስርሑ ንኣምላኽ ከኽብር ወሰነ። እዚ ውሳኔ — ኣብ ዓሚቝ ስቓይ ዝተወሰደ — ኣምላኽ ብኣኡ ዝሃንጾ ኩሉ መሰረት ክኸውን ክረጋገጽ ነበሮ።
(Ab Gibtsi Yosef ni'Potifar, haleqa halewa Fir'on — hayaln habtamn seb siltan — teshete.)
Scene 9 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph managing Potiphar's grand household efficiently - overseeing servants, reviewing scrolls, and organizing supplies. The household prospering visibly under his care. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
The Lord was with Joseph, and everything he touched prospered. Potiphar noticed that his household flourished under Joseph's management — crops yielded more, servants worked better, and affairs ran smoothly. He promoted Joseph to overseer of his entire estate, entrusting everything he owned to Joseph's care. Potiphar did not concern himself with anything except the food he ate. God's blessing on Joseph was so evident that even a pagan master recognized it. Faithfulness in small things always opens doors to greater responsibility.
እግዚኣብሄር ምስ ዮሴፍ ነበረ፡ ዝተንከፎ ኩሉ ተዓወተ። ጶጢፋር ገዝኡ ብምሕደራ ዮሴፍ ከም ዝዕምብብ ኣስተውዓለ — ዓጺድ ዝያዳ ፈረየ፡ ኣገልገልቲ ብዝሓሸ ሰርሑ፡ ጕዳያት ብስሉጥ ከደ። ንዮሴፍ ኣብ ልዕሊ ኩሉ ንብረቱ ሓላፊ ገበሮ፡ ኩሉ ዘለዎ ንዮሴፍ ኣረከቦ። ጶጢፋር ብዘይ ካብ ዝበልዖ ምግቢ ብኻልእ ነገር ኣይተሻቐለን። በረኸት ኣምላኽ ኣብ ዮሴፍ ክሳብ ጻድቃዊ ዘይኮነ ጐይታ'ውን ዝለለዮ ብሩህ ነበረ። ኣብ ንኡስ ነገራት ምእማን ኩሉ ግዜ ናብ ዝዓበየ ሓላፍነት ማዕጾ ይኸፍት።
(Egzi'abher mis Yosef nebere, zitenkifo kulu te'awete.)
Scene 10 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph turning away and fleeing from Potiphar's wife who reaches for his garment. Joseph running toward an open doorway, choosing integrity over temptation. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Joseph was handsome and well-built, and Potiphar's wife took notice. Day after day, she tried to seduce him, but Joseph consistently refused. "My master trusts me with everything in this house," he told her. "How could I do such a wicked thing and sin against God?" One day she grabbed his cloak, but Joseph fled, leaving the garment in her hand. He chose integrity over comfort, knowing that momentary pleasure could destroy the character God was building in him. True strength is not in yielding to desire, but in the courage to flee from it.
ዮሴፍ ጽቡቕ ትርኢትን ጽፉፍ ቅርጽን ነበሮ፡ ሰበይቲ ጶጢፋር ድማ ኣስተውዓለቶ። መዓልቲ ብመዓልቲ ከተፈታትኖ ፈተነት፡ ግን ዮሴፍ ብቐጻሊ ኣበየ። "ጐይታይ ኣብዚ ገዛ ብኹሉ ይኣምነኒ" በላ። "ከመይ ገይረ ከምዚ ዝኣመሰለ ክፉእ ነገር ገይረ ንኣምላኽ ክሓጥእ?" ሓደ መዓልቲ ቀሚሹ ሓዘት፡ ግን ዮሴፍ ሃዲሙ ክዳኑ ኣብ ኢዳ ገዲፉ። ንጽህና ኣብ ልዕሊ ምቾት መረጸ፡ ናይ ህሞት ደስታ ኣምላኽ ኣባኡ ዝሃንጾ ባህሪ ከጥፍእ ከም ዝኽእል ፈሊጡ። ሓቀኛ ሓይሊ ንድሌት ምስዓር ኣይኮነን፡ ካብኡ ንምህዳም ትብዓት እዩ።

Scene 11 of 25
[PICTURE: Potiphar's wife holding Joseph's garment and accusing him before angry household servants, while Joseph is being led away in chains toward prison. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Humiliated by Joseph's rejection, Potiphar's wife twisted the truth into a weapon. She held up Joseph's garment and accused him of the very sin he had fled from. Potiphar, furious, had Joseph thrown into prison — the royal dungeon where the king's prisoners were confined. Joseph had done everything right and was punished for it. This is one of life's hardest lessons: doing the right thing does not guarantee immediate reward. Sometimes integrity leads to suffering before it leads to vindication. But God never wastes our pain.
ብነጸግታ ዮሴፍ ዝተዋረደት ሰበይቲ ጶጢፋር ሓቂ ናብ ኣጽዋር ቀየረታ። ቀሚሽ ዮሴፍ ኣልዒላ ነቲ ዝሃደመሉ ሓጢኣት ባዕሉ ከሲሳቶ። ጶጢፋር ተቖጢዑ ንዮሴፍ ናብ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ኣእተዎ — እሱራት ንጉስ ዝሕብሱሉ ንጉሳዊ ቤት ማእሰርቲ። ዮሴፍ ኩሉ ቅኑዕ ገይሩ ብሰንኩ ተቐጸዐ። እዚ ካብ ዝኸበዱ ትምህርትታት ህይወት ሓደ እዩ፡ ቅኑዕ ምግባር ቅጽበታዊ ሽልማት ኣየረጋግጽን። ሓደ ሓደ ግዜ ንጽህና ቅድሚ ናብ ጽድቂ ምምራሕ ናብ ስቓይ ይመርሕ። ግን ኣምላኽ ቃንዛና ኣየባኽኖን።

Even in prison, Joseph refused to surrender to despair. The Lord remained with him and granted him favor in the eyes of the prison warden. Once again, Joseph's character shone through adversity. The warden put Joseph in charge of all the other prisoners, and everything in the prison was managed under Joseph's direction. The pattern was clear: wherever Joseph went, whatever his circumstances, God's presence and Joseph's faithfulness combined to produce excellence. Prison could confine his body but not his spirit or his God-given purpose.
ኣብ ቤት ማእሰርቲ'ውን ዮሴፍ ንተስፋ ምቝራጽ ክስዓር ኣበየ። እግዚኣብሄር ምስኡ ኣቕለ ኣብ ዓይኒ ሓላዊ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ሞገስ ሃቦ። ከም ብሓድሽ ባህሪ ዮሴፍ ብጸገም ኣብረቐ። ሓላዊ ንዮሴፍ ኣብ ልዕሊ ኹሎም እሱራት ሓላፊ ገበሮ፡ ኣብ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ዘሎ ኩሉ ብምሕደራ ዮሴፍ ይመሓደር ነበረ። ኣገባብ ብሩህ ነበረ፡ ዮሴፍ ኣብ ዝኸደ ዝኾነ ኩነታቱ ይኹን፡ ህላወ ኣምላኽን ምእማን ዮሴፍን ተጸንቢሮም ብሉጽነት ኣፍረዩ። ቤት ማእሰርቲ ሰብነቱ ክእስር ይኽእል ግን መንፈሱ ወይ ኣምላኽ ዝሃቦ ዕላማ ኣይኮነን።

Scene 13 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph sitting with two troubled men - Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker - in prison, listening intently to their dreams. Both men look worried and confused. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Two of Pharaoh's officials — the chief cupbearer and the chief baker — were thrown into the same prison after offending the king. One morning, Joseph noticed they both looked troubled. "Why are your faces so sad today?" he asked. They explained they had each dreamed a vivid dream but had no one to interpret them. Joseph replied with humble confidence: "Do not interpretations belong to God? Tell me your dreams." Even in his own suffering, Joseph noticed the pain of others and offered to help — a mark of true character.
ክልተ ሰብ ስልጣን ፈርኦን — ኣኽባሪ ጽዋእን ሓላቕ ሰንካቲትን — ንንጉስ ድሕሪ ምግሃስ ናብ ተመሳሳሊ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ተደርበዩ። ሓደ ንግሆ ዮሴፍ ክልቲኦም ዝተሻቐሉ ከም ዝመስሉ ኣስተውዓለ። "ስለምንታይ ገጽኩም ሎሚ ዝሓዘነ ይመስል?" ኢሉ ሓተቶም። ነፍሲ ወከፎም ንጹር ሕልሚ ከም ዝሓለሙ ግን ዝትርጉመሎም ከም ዘየልቦ ገለጹ። ዮሴፍ ብትሑት ትብዓት መለሰ፡ "ትርጓመ ናይ ኣምላኽ ደይኮነን? ሕልምኹም ንገሩኒ።" ኣብ ገዛእ ስቓዩ'ውን ዮሴፍ ቃንዛ ካልኦት ኣስተውዒሉ ክሕግዝ ኣቕረበ — ምልክት ናይ ሓቀኛ ባህሪ።
(Kilte seb siltan Fir'on — akbari tsiwa'in halaq senkatitin — ninigus dihri mighas nab temesasali bet ma'eserti tederbeyyu.)
Scene 14 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph interpreting dreams - on one side showing a vine with three branches bearing grapes (cupbearer's dream), on the other side three baskets with birds eating from them (baker's dream). East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
God gave Joseph the interpretation of both dreams. The cupbearer's dream of a vine with three branches meant that in three days he would be restored to his position, serving Pharaoh wine again. But the baker's dream of three baskets with birds eating from the top basket carried a darker meaning — in three days, Pharaoh would execute him. Both interpretations came true exactly as Joseph had said. Joseph asked only one thing of the cupbearer: "Remember me when things go well for you. Mention me to Pharaoh and get me out of this prison." But the cupbearer forgot.
ኣምላኽ ንዮሴፍ ትርጓመ ክልቲኡ ሕልምታት ሃቦ። ናይ ኣኽባሪ ጽዋእ ሕልሚ ናይ ሰለስተ ጨናፍር ዘለዎ ተኽሊ ወይኒ ኣብ ሰለስተ መዓልቲ ናብ ስልጣኑ ተመሊሱ ንፈርኦን ወይኒ ከገልግል ማለት ነበረ። ግን ናይ ሰንካቲት ሕልሚ ናይ ሰለስተ ቅርጫት ኣዕዋፍ ካብ ላዕለዋይ ቅርጫት ዝበልዓ ጸልማት ትርጉም ነበሮ — ኣብ ሰለስተ መዓልቲ ፈርኦን ክቐትሎ እዩ። ክልቲኡ ትርጓመታት ከም ዮሴፍ ዝበሎ ብልክዕ ተፈጸመ። ዮሴፍ ንኣኽባሪ ጽዋእ ሓንቲ ነገር ጥራይ ሓተቶ፡ "ጽቡቕ ምስ ዝኾነልካ ዘክረኒ። ንፈርኦን ብዛዕባይ ንገሮ ካብዚ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ኣውጽኣኒ።" ግን ኣኽባሪ ጽዋእ ረሰዐ።
(Amlakh ni'Yosef tirgwame kilt'itu hilmitat habo.)
Scene 15 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph alone in his prison cell, two years passing shown by marks on the wall. His expression shows patience mixed with longing, but not despair. A faint divine light surrounds him. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Two full years passed. Joseph remained in prison, forgotten by the cupbearer, seemingly forgotten by the world. These were years of testing — years when faith could have easily crumbled. Yet Joseph persevered, continuing to trust that God had not abandoned him even when evidence suggested otherwise. Sometimes God's timing feels unbearably slow to us, but He is always working behind the scenes, arranging circumstances with precision we cannot see. The delay was not God's neglect; it was preparation for a moment that had not yet arrived.
ምሉእ ክልተ ዓመት ሓለፈ። ዮሴፍ ኣብ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ተረፈ፡ ብኣኽባሪ ጽዋእ ዝተረስዐ፡ ብዓለም ዝተረስዐ ዝመስል። እዚኣተን ናይ ፈተና ዓመታት ነበራ — እምነት ቀሊሉ ክፈርስ ዝኽእለለን ዓመታት። ይኹን'ምበር ዮሴፍ ጸንዐ፡ ኣምላኽ ኣይሓደጎን ኢሉ ክኣምን ቀጸለ ምስክርነት ብኣንጻሩ ይሕብር እንተ ዝመስል'ውን። ሓደ ሓደ ግዜ ግዜ ኣምላኽ ንዓና ዘይጻወር ዝሑል ይስመዓና፡ ግን ኩሉ ግዜ ኣብ ድሕሪ መድረኽ ይሰርሕ፡ ክንርእዮ ዘይንኽእል ዕቱብነት ኩነታት ይሰርዕ። ምድንጓይ ድንቁርና ኣምላኽ ኣይነበረን፡ ገና ዘይበጽሐ ንህሞት ምድላው ነበረ።
(Milu'e kilte amet haleffe. Yosef ab bet ma'eserti terefe, bi'akbari tsiwa'e ziterese'e.)
Scene 16 of 25
[PICTURE: Pharaoh tossing restlessly in his ornate bed, haunted by dreams of seven fat cows being devoured by seven thin cows, and seven full ears of grain consumed by seven thin ears. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Then Pharaoh himself had two disturbing dreams that none of his magicians or wise men could interpret. In the first, seven fat, healthy cows emerged from the Nile, followed by seven gaunt, ugly cows that devoured them. In the second, seven plump heads of grain grew on a single stalk, then seven thin, scorched heads swallowed them up. Pharaoh was deeply troubled — he sensed these dreams carried a message of great importance. At last, the cupbearer remembered Joseph. "There was a Hebrew prisoner who interpreted my dream perfectly," he told Pharaoh. God's timing had arrived.
ድሕሪኡ ፈርኦን ባዕሉ ክልተ ዘሻቕሉ ሕልምታት ሓለመ ዝኾነ ካብ ጠንቆልቱ ወይ ጥበበኛታቱ ክትርጉም ዘይከኣለ። ኣብ ቀዳማይ ሸውዓተ ስቡሓትን ጥዕየኛታትን ላሕምታት ካብ ኒል ወጻ ስዒቡ ሸውዓተ ዝዓጸፋን ዘሰክሓን ላሕምታት ብልዐአን። ኣብ ካልኣይ ሸውዓተ ጻዕዳውን ስጡሓትን ርእሲ ስርናይ ኣብ ሓደ ገፈር ዓበየ ድሕሪኡ ሸውዓተ ቀጢናትን ዝነደዳን ርእስታት ውሓጠአን። ፈርኦን ኣዝዩ ተሻቐለ — እዞም ሕልምታት ዓቢ ኣገዳስነት ዘለዎ መልእኽቲ ከም ዝሓዙ ተሰመዖ። ኣብ መወዳእታ ኣኽባሪ ጽዋእ ንዮሴፍ ዘከሮ። "ሕልመይ ብልክዕ ዝተርጐመ ናይ ዕብራዊ እሱር ነበረ" ንፈርኦን ነገሮ። ግዜ ኣምላኽ በጺሑ ነበረ።
(Dihr'iu Fir'on ba'lu kilte zeshaqilu hilmitat haleme zikone kab tenqoltu wey tibebeňatatu kitrigum zeyk'ale.)
Scene 17 of 25
[PICTURE: Joseph, freshly shaved and in clean Egyptian garments, standing before Pharaoh on his grand throne. Joseph speaking with calm authority while Pharaoh listens intently. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Joseph was rushed from the dungeon. He shaved, changed his clothes, and was brought before Pharaoh. When Pharaoh said, "I have heard you can interpret dreams," Joseph immediately gave God the credit: "I cannot do it, but God will give Pharaoh the answer he desires." Joseph explained that both dreams shared one message: God was revealing what He was about to do. Seven years of unprecedented abundance would come to Egypt, followed by seven years of devastating famine so severe it would consume all memory of the plenty. Joseph spoke with authority that could only come from God — and Pharaoh recognized it.

God had been training Joseph all along, using every painful experience as preparation for this monumental task.

Scene 24 of 25

Years later, after Jacob died, the brothers feared Joseph would finally take revenge. They sent a message claiming their father had asked Joseph to forgive them. They fell before him, saying, "We are your slaves." But Joseph wept at their fear and spoke words that echo through all of history: "Don't be afraid. Am I in the place of God? You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good, to accomplish what is now being done — the saving of many lives." Joseph understood that God's sovereignty does not excuse human sin, but it can redeem it. Every act of evil against him had been woven into God's tapestry of salvation.

Joseph's story is one of the most powerful testimonies of God's faithfulness in all of Scripture. A favored son became a slave, a slave became a prisoner, and a prisoner became the second most powerful man in the world — all according to God's perfect plan. Joseph endured betrayal, false accusation, and years of forgotten imprisonment, yet he never abandoned his faith or his integrity. His life teaches us that God is sovereign over every circumstance, that character built in darkness shines brightest in the light, and that true forgiveness has the power to restore what was broken. No matter what you face today, God is writing a story with your life that is bigger than your current chapter.

The Moral of the Story / ትምህርቲ ናይዚ ዛንታ

God can use our worst experiences for His greater purpose. Joseph's journey from the pit to the palace was not despite his suffering — it was through it. Every painful chapter was preparation for the role God had planned for him.

Forgiveness is powerful. Joseph had every right to seek revenge, but he chose to release his brothers from the debt of their sin. Forgiveness freed not only his brothers but Joseph himself from the prison of bitterness.

Stay faithful to God even when life is unfair. Joseph was obedient, moral, and hardworking — and was still punished. But his faithfulness in unjust circumstances built the character that qualified him for greatness.

God is sovereign over all circumstances. Neither human evil nor unjust systems could derail God's plan. What people intended for harm, God redirected for the salvation of nations.

Discussion Questions / ናይ ምይይጥ ሕቶታት

1. Joseph was faithful in every position — slave, prisoner, and ruler. How does your attitude toward small responsibilities reflect the kind of leader you might become? What does it mean to be faithful when no one is watching?

2. When Joseph was tempted by Potiphar's wife, he fled rather than fighting the temptation. In what situations might running away actually be the bravest choice? How can you prepare yourself in advance for moments of intense temptation?

3. Joseph waited thirteen years between receiving God's promise (his dreams) and seeing it fulfilled. Have you ever had to wait a long time for something you believed God promised? How do you maintain faith when God seems silent?

4. Joseph chose to forgive brothers who showed no remorse for over twenty years. Is forgiveness conditional on the other person apologizing? What does unforgiveness do to the person holding onto it? How is Joseph's forgiveness a picture of how God forgives us?

5. Joseph said God "meant it for good" — but does that mean God causes evil things to happen to us? How do you understand the difference between God causing suffering and God using suffering? How does this change how you view your own difficult experiences?
