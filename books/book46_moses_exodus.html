Scene 1
[PICTURE: Moses, now eighty years old with a weathered face and shepherd's staff, standing at the edge of the desert looking toward Egypt in the distance. Behind him, sheep graze peacefully. Forty years had passed since Moses fled Egypt as a fugitive. The prince who once commanded armies was now an eighty-year-old shepherd in Midian's wilderness — forgotten by the world, but not by God. After the burning bush, after every excuse he could offer, Moses finally turned his face back toward Egypt. He carried nothing impressive — just a wooden staff and a divine commission. God's timing rarely makes sense to us. Moses probably wondered why God waited four decades while His people suffered. But God was preparing both the deliverer and the moment.
ሙሴ ከም ህዱም ካብ ግብጺ ካብ ዝሃድም ኣርብዓ ዓመታት ሓሊፉ ነበረ። እቲ ሓደ ግዜ ሰራዊት ዝእዝዝ ዝነበረ ስዉር ሕጂ ናይ ሰማንያ ዓመት ጓሳ ኣብ በረኻ ሚድያን'ዩ ዝነበረ — ብዓለም ዝተረስዐ ግን ብኣምላኽ ዘይተረስዐ። ድሕሪ እቲ ዝነድድ ቆጽሊ ድሕሪ ኩሉ ዝሃቦ ምኽንያት ሙሴ ኣብ መወዳእታ ገጹ ናብ ግብጺ መለሰ። ዘደንቕ ነገር ኣይሓዘን — ናይ ዕንጨይቲ በትሪ ከምኡውን ናይ ኣምላኽ ተልእኾ ጥራይ። ናይ ኣምላኽ ግዜ ንዓና ዋሕዚ ዘይብሉ'ዩ። ሙሴ ህዝቡ ክሳቐ ከሎ ኣምላኽ ስለምንታይ ኣርባዕተ ዓሰርተ ዓመት ዝጸንሐ ሓሲቡ ክኸውን ይኽእል። ግን ኣምላኽ ነቲ መድሕንን ነቲ ህሞትን ይዳሎ ነበረ።

Scene 2
[PICTURE: Moses and his brother Aaron standing together at the gates of Pharaoh's grand palace. Egyptian guards with spears flank the massive doors. Moses and Aaron walked into the throne room of the most powerful ruler on earth. The message they carried was simple and uncompromising: "Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel: Let my people go." Imagine the audacity — two elderly men from a slave nation, demanding that Pharaoh release his entire workforce. They had no army, no political leverage, no wealth. They had only the word of God. Sometimes obedience looks foolish to the world. But the power behind God's word doesn't depend on the impressiveness of the messenger.
ሙሴን ኣሮንን ናብ ቤት ዝፋን ናይ ኣብ ምድሪ ዝሓየለ ገዛኢ ኣተዉ። ዝሓዝዎ መልእኽቲ ቀሊልን ዘይድንግጽን ነበረ፡ "ኣምላኽ እስራኤል እግዚኣብሄር ከምዚ ይብል፡ ህዝበይ ሕደጎም ይኺዱ።" ነቲ ድፍረት ሕሰብ — ክልተ ኣረጋውያን ካብ ባርያ ሃገር ፈርኦን ምሉእ ሓይሊ ስራሑ ክሓድግ ዝሓቱ። ሰራዊት ኣይነበሮምን ፖለቲካዊ ጽልዋ ኣይነበሮምን ሃብቲ'ውን ኣይነበሮምን። ቃል ኣምላኽ ጥራይ'ዩ ዝነበሮም። ሓደ ሓደ ግዜ ተኣዛዝነት ንዓለም ዕሽነት ይመስላ። ግን ድሕሪ ቃል ኣምላኽ ዘሎ ሓይሊ ኣብ ምልክዕነት ናይቲ መልእኽተኛ ኣይምርኮስን።
(Muse'n Aron'n nab bet zifan nay ab midri zihayele gezai atewu. Zihazwo mel'ikhti qelil'n zeyidingits'n nebere, "Amlak Isra'el Igzi'abhier kemzi yibil, hizibey hidegom yikhidu." Neti difret hiseb — kilte aregawyan kab barya hager Feri'on milu'e hayli sirahu kihadig zihhatu. Serawit ayneberomin polettikawi tsilwa ayneberomin habti'wun ayneberomin. Qal Amlak tiray'yu zineborom. Hade hade gize te'azazinet ni'alem ishnet yimesla. Gin dihri qal Amlak zelo hayli ab milik'inet nayti mel'ikhtegna ayimirkosin.)
Scene 3
[PICTURE: Pharaoh sitting arrogantly on his golden throne, laughing dismissively at Moses and Aaron. His face shows contempt and mockery. Egyptian advisors smirk behind him. Pharaoh waves his hand in rejection, his crown gleaming with jewels. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Pharaoh's response was contemptuous: "Who is the Lord, that I should obey Him? I do not know the Lord and I will not let Israel go." Then he made things worse — he increased the slaves' workload, forcing them to make bricks without straw. Pharaoh's strategy was deliberate: crush the people's hope so they would blame Moses rather than follow him. This is how oppressive systems work — they punish the oppressed for daring to ask for freedom, hoping that suffering will silence the cry for justice.
ናይ ፈርኦን መልሲ ናይ ንዕቀት ነበረ፡ "ክእዘዞ እግዚኣብሄር መን'ዩ? ንእግዚኣብሄር ኣይፈልጦን እስራኤል'ውን ኣይሓድጎምን።" ድሕሪኡ ነገራት ኣብኣሰ — ብዘይ ሳዕሪ ጡብ ክሰርሑ ብምግዳድ ናይ ባሮት ስራሕ ወሰኸ። ስትራተጂ ፈርኦን ዕላማ ዝነበሮ'ዩ፡ ተስፋ ህዝቢ ስበር ንሙሴ ክስዕቡ ዘይኮነ ክወቕስዎ። ጨቋኒ ስርዓታት ከምዚ'ዮም ዝሰርሑ — ናጽነት ንምሕታት ዝደፈሩ ጭቁናት ይቐጽዑ ስቓይ ነቲ ናይ ፍትሒ ጸውዒት ከም ዝስቅ ተስፋ ብምግባር።
(Nay Feri'on melsi nay ni'iqet nebere, "Ki'izezo Igzi'abhier men'yu? Ni'Igzi'abhier ayifelton Isra'el'wun ayihhadigomon." Dihri'u negeraat ab'ase — bizey sa'iri tub kisirhu bimiggidad nay barot sirah wesekhe. Stratej Feri'on ilama zinebero'yu; tesfa hizbi sber ni'Muse kisi'ibu zeyikone kiwqiswo. Cheqwani sir'atat kemzi'yom zisirhu — natsinet nimihhitat zidiferu chiqqunat yiqets'u siqay neti nay fithi tsew'it kem zisiq tesfa bimigbar.)

God's response to Pharaoh's arrogance was methodical and devastating. The first plague struck at the heart of Egypt's identity — the Nile, worshipped as the god Hapi, turned to blood. Fish died, the water stank, and Egypt's source of life became a river of death. Each plague was not random; it was a direct confrontation with a specific Egyptian deity. God was not merely punishing Egypt — He was dismantling their entire belief system, proving that every god they trusted was powerless before Him. The Nile they worshipped could not even protect itself.
ናይ ኣምላኽ መልሲ ንትዕቢት ፈርኦን ስርዓታዊን ዘጥፍእን ነበረ። እታ ቀዳመይቲ መዓት ናብ ልቢ መንነት ግብጺ ወቕዐት — ኒል ከም ኣምላኽ ሃፒ ዝምለኽ ዝነበረ ናብ ደም ተለወጠ። ዓሳ ሞተ ማይ ሸተተ ናይ ግብጺ ምንጪ ሂወት ናይ ሞት ወንዝ ኮነ። ነፍሲ ወከፍ መዓት ብዘይ ምኽንያት ኣይነበረን፣ ምስ ፍሉይ ኣምላኽ ግብጺ ቀጥታዊ ግጥም'ዩ ዝነበረ። ኣምላኽ ንግብጺ ይቐጽዕ ጥራይ ኣይነበረን — ምሉእ ናይ እምነት ስርዓቶም የፍርስ ነበረ ኩሉ ዝኣመንዎ ኣምላኽ ኣብ ቅድሚኡ ሓይሊ ዘይብሉ ምዃኑ ይረጋግጽ ነበረ። ዝምለኽዎ ዝነበሩ ኒል ርእሱ'ውን ክከላኸል ኣይከኣለን።

Scene 5
[PICTURE: A terrifying scene of frogs covering everything in Egypt — on beds, in ovens, on people. Egyptians swat desperately at swarms of gnats and flies. Pharaoh sits on his throne covered in frogs, looking disgusted but still defiant. Moses watches calmly. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Plague after plague descended on Egypt — frogs overran every home, gnats covered every person and animal, swarms of flies blackened the sky. With each plague, Pharaoh would beg Moses to pray for relief, promising to release Israel. But each time the suffering stopped, Pharaoh hardened his heart again. This is the terrifying pattern of a hardened heart: temporary remorse during pain, followed by the same stubbornness once comfort returns. Pharaoh's story warns us that every time we resist God's voice, our hearts grow a little harder, until we can no longer hear Him at all.
መዓት ድሕሪ መዓት ኣብ ግብጺ ወረደ — ቆቋሕ ኩሉ ገዛ ኣዕረበ ቃንጫ ኩሉ ሰብን እንስሳን ሸፈነ ኣንራሕ ሰማይ ኣጸልመተ። ብነፍሲ ወከፍ መዓት ፈርኦን ሙሴ ንእስራኤል ክሓድጎም ብምምባል ንዕረፍቲ ክጽሊ ለሚኑ። ግን ስቓይ ኩሉ ግዜ ምስ ደው በለ ፈርኦን ልቡ ከም ብሓድሽ ኣትረረ። እዚ'ዩ እቲ ዘፍርህ ቅዲ ናይ ዝረረ ልቢ፡ ኣብ ግዜ ቃንዛ ግዝያዊ ጣዕሳ ራህዋ ምስ ተመልሰ ድማ ተመሳሳሊ ትሪ። ዛንታ ፈርኦን ነፍሲ ወከፍ ግዜ ድምጺ ኣምላኽ ምስ እንቃወም ልብና ቁሩብ ይረርር ክሳብ ፈጺምና ክንሰምዖ ዘይንኽእል ንኸውን የጠንቅቐና።

Scene 6

The plagues intensified — livestock disease killed Egypt's cattle while Israel's animals remained untouched. Painful boils erupted on every Egyptian. Hail mixed with fire destroyed their crops. Locusts devoured whatever remained. Three days of impenetrable darkness covered Egypt — so thick people could not see each other — yet the Israelites had light in their dwellings. God was making an undeniable distinction between His people and their oppressors. This was not random suffering; it was targeted, purposeful, and increasingly severe. God's patience has limits, and His justice, when it finally moves, is thorough.
መዓታት ተበርትዑ — ሕማም እንስሳ ናይ ግብጺ ከብቲ ቀተለ ናይ እስራኤል እንስሳ ግን ዘይተተንከፈ ጸንሐ። ቃንዛ ዝህብ ቁስሊ ኣብ ኩሉ ግብጻዊ ወጽአ። ምስ ሓዊ ዝተሓወሰ በረድ ዘርኦም ኣጥፍኤ። ኣንበጣ ዝተረፈ ኩሉ በልዐ። ሰለስተ መዓልቲ ዘይሰላገም ጸልማት ንግብጺ ሸፈና — ሰባት ንሓድሕዶም ክረኣኣዩ ዘይክእሉ ክንድኡ ጽልግልግ — ግን እስራኤላውያን ኣብ ገዝኦም ብርሃን ነበሮም። ኣምላኽ ኣብ መንጎ ህዝቡን ጨቋኒኦምን ዘይክሓድ ፍልልይ ይገብር ነበረ። እዚ ብዘይ ምኽንያት ስቓይ ኣይነበረን፣ ዕላማ ዝነበሮ ዓላማዊ ክብደቱ'ውን ዝውስኽ ነበረ። ትዕግስቲ ኣምላኽ ደረት ኣለዎ ፍትሑ ኣብ መወዳእታ ምስ ተንቀሳቐሰ ድማ ምሉእ'ዩ።

Anbeta ziterefe kulu beli'e. Seleste me'alti zeyselegem tselmat ni'Gibitsi shefena — sebat nihadihdom kire'a'ayu zeyikhilu kindawu tsilgiligig — gin Isra'elawyan ab gez'om birhan neberom. Before the final plague, Moses stood before Pharaoh one last time. This was not cruelty for cruelty's sake. For generations, Egypt had murdered Hebrew sons, throwing babies into the Nile. Pharaoh had been given nine chances to repent, nine demonstrations of God's power, nine opportunities to choose mercy. He chose stubbornness every time. The consequences of sustained rebellion against God are devastating — not because God is vengeful, but because He is just.

God gave Israel specific instructions for the Passover: each family must take a perfect lamb, slaughter it at twilight, and paint its blood on their doorposts and lintel. They must roast it whole and eat it with bitter herbs and unleavened bread — dressed and ready to leave, sandals on their feet, staff in hand. The blood was the sign. When the destroyer passed through Egypt, he would see the blood and pass over that house. This was not magic or ritual — it was obedience and faith made visible. God was teaching His people that deliverance always costs something. Freedom requires sacrifice. An innocent life had to be given so that the guilty could live.

At midnight, the Lord struck down every firstborn in Egypt. From Pharaoh's palace to the darkest dungeon, death visited every Egyptian home. A great cry rose across the land — a wail of grief unlike anything ever heard before or since. But in Goshen, where blood marked the doorposts, every child slept safely. The difference between life and death that night was not wealth, power, intelligence, or luck. It was simply obedience — trusting God's word enough to act on it. The blood didn't make logical sense to human reasoning, but faith doesn't require complete understanding. It requires trust.

The Israelites didn't leave Egypt poor or empty-handed — God ensured they received back payment for generations of unpaid labor.

God did not simply liberate His people and abandon them to figure out the journey alone. He went before them — a pillar of cloud by day to guide their path, and a pillar of fire by night to give them light and warmth. The Moral of the Story

God hears the cries of the oppressed and acts — even when deliverance takes longer than we expect. True freedom requires leaving the familiar behind, even when the unknown is terrifying. God's timing is perfect even when it seems unbearably late; He prepares both the deliverer and the moment. Obedience in the face of impossible odds is what faith looks like — stepping forward when logic says retreat. The same God who delivers also guides and provides for every step of the journey. Freedom is not merely escape from bondage; it is walking into the purpose God has for your life.
