Jeremiah: The Weeping Prophet

Faithful When It Costs Everything

Ages 10-17

Written by Daniel Tesfamariam

Key Verse: Jeremiah 1:5 — "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart."

Scene 1
[PICTURE: A young boy, perhaps 15 years old, kneeling in a simple room as divine light streams down upon him. God's hand reaches from heaven toward the boy. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

In the small village of Anathoth, near Jerusalem, a young boy named Jeremiah knelt in prayer. He came from a family of priests and had grown up knowing God. But nothing could have prepared him for what happened next.

The voice of the Lord came to him: "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart; I appointed you as a prophet to the nations."

Jeremiah trembled. God had known him — truly known him — before he even existed. His life had purpose and meaning before his first breath. He was not an accident. He was chosen.

ኣብ ናእሽቶ ዓዲ ዓናቶት፡ ኣብ ጥቓ ኢየሩሳሌም፡ ሓደ ንእሽቶ ቆልዓ ስሙ ኤርምያስ ተምበርኪኹ ይጽሊ ነበረ። ካብ ስድራ ቤት ካህናት ዝመጸ ኮይኑ ንኣምላኽ ፈሊጡ ዓቢዩ። ግን ንዝሰዓበ ነገር ዝተዳለወ ኣይነበረን።

ቃል እግዚኣብሄር ናብኡ መጸ፡ "ኣብ ማህጸን ከይፈጠርኩኻ ፈሊጠካ ነይረ፡ ከይተወለድካ ከኣ ቀዲሰካ ነይረ፤ ንህዝብታት ነቢይ ክትከውን ሸይመካ።"

(Ab na'ishto adi Anatot, ab tsiga Iyerusalem, hade ni'ishto qol'a simu Ermyas temberkikhu yitseli nebere.)
Scene 2
[PICTURE: The young boy Jeremiah with hands raised defensively, looking overwhelmed and afraid, while a gentle divine glow surrounds him reassuringly. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

Jeremiah's first response was fear. "Alas, Sovereign LORD!" he cried. "I do not know how to speak; I am too young!"

He was perhaps only fifteen years old. How could he stand before kings and priests and deliver God's message? He was just a boy. The task seemed impossibly large for someone so small and inexperienced.

But God's answer was immediate and firm: "Do not say, 'I am too young.' You must go to everyone I send you to and say whatever I command you. Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you and will rescue you."

God did not dismiss Jeremiah's youth — He simply declared it irrelevant. When God calls, age is not a barrier.

ናይ ኤርምያስ ቀዳማይ መልሲ ፍርሒ ነበረ። "ወይለይ ልዑል እግዚኣብሄር!" ኢሉ ጸውዐ። "ክዛረብ ኣይፈልጥን፣ ንእሽቶ እየ!"

ግን መልሲ ኣምላኽ ቅልጡፍን ጽኑዕን ነበረ፡ "ንእሽቶ እየ ኣይትበል። ናብ ዝልእኸካ ኹሉ ክትከይድ ኣለካ፡ ዝእዝዘካ ኹሉ ድማ ክትዛረብ ኣለካ። ኣይትፍራሆም፡ ምሳኻ ስለ ዘለኹ ከድሕነካ እየ።"

(Na'i Ermyas qedamay melsi firihi nebere. "Weyley li'ul Egzi'abher!" ilu tsew'e. "Kizareb ayfeltin, ni'ishto iye!")
Scene 3
[PICTURE: A divine hand gently touching the lips of the young boy Jeremiah, with golden light flowing from the touch. The boy's eyes are wide with awe. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

Then the LORD reached out His hand and touched Jeremiah's mouth. The boy felt warmth spread through him like fire and honey combined — both burning and sweet.

"I have put my words in your mouth," God declared. "See, today I appoint you over nations and kingdoms to uproot and tear down, to destroy and overthrow, to build and to plant."

This was the moment everything changed. Jeremiah's words would no longer be merely his own. When he spoke, he would carry the very words of God. This was both the greatest honor and the heaviest burden a person could bear.

ሽዑ እግዚኣብሄር ኢዱ ዘርጊሑ ኣፍ ኤርምያስ ተንከፈ። እቲ ቆልዓ ከም ሓዊን መዓርን ዝተሓወሰ ምውቐት ተሰማዖ — ዝነድድን ዝጥዕምን።

"ቃለይ ኣብ ኣፍካ ኣንቢረ" ኢሉ ኣምላኽ ኣወጀ። "ሎሚ ኣብ ልዕሊ ህዝብታትን መንግስትታትን ሸይመካ ኣለኹ — ክትምሕው ክትፈርስ ክትዕንው ክትድርብን ክትሃንጽ ክትተክልን።"

(Shi'u Egzi'abher idu zergihu af Ermyas tenkefe. Iti qol'a kem hawin me'arin zitehawise miwqet tesema'o.)
Scene 4
[PICTURE: Young Jeremiah seeing two visions side by side - an almond tree branch with white blossoms on one side, and a boiling pot tilting from the north on the other. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

God gave Jeremiah two visions to confirm his calling. First: "What do you see, Jeremiah?" The boy answered, "I see the branch of an almond tree." God replied, "You have seen correctly, for I am watching to see that my word is fulfilled."

The Hebrew word for almond sounds like "watching." God was telling Jeremiah: I am always awake, always watching, always keeping my promises — both for blessing and for judgment.

Then came the second vision: "What do you see?" Jeremiah said, "I see a boiling pot, tilting away from the north." God explained that disaster would come from the north upon all who lived in Judah because they had abandoned Him for false gods.

These two visions would define Jeremiah's entire ministry: God watches faithfully, and judgment is coming for the unfaithful.

ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ ክልተ ራእይ ሃቦ ጻውዒቱ ንምርግጋጽ። ቀዳማይ፡ "እንታይ ትርኢ ኤርምያስ?" እቲ ቆልዓ "ጨንፈር ናይ ሎዝ ኦም ይርኢ ኣለኹ" ኢሉ መለሰ። ኣምላኽ ድማ "ጽቡቕ ሪኢኻ፡ ቃለይ ንምፍጻም ይሕሉ ኣለኹ" ኢሉ መለሰ።

ካልኣይ ራእይ ድማ መጸ፡ "እንታይ ትርኢ?" ኤርምያስ "ዝፈልሕ ድስቲ ካብ ሰሜን ዝዝንብል ይርኢ ኣለኹ" በለ። ኣምላኽ ካብ ሰሜን ጥፍኣት ከም ዝመጽእ ገለጸ።

(Amlak ni'Ermyas kilte ra'iy habo tsaw'itu nimriggatsi. Qedamay: "Intay tri'i Ermyas?" Iti qol'a "Chenfer nay loz om yir'i aleku" ilu melese.)
Scene 5
[PICTURE: Young Jeremiah standing tall with a scroll in hand, behind him scenes of both destruction (crumbling walls) and new growth (planted seedlings). A divine mandate visual. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

God's commission to Jeremiah was unlike any other prophet's calling. He was appointed "over nations and kingdoms" — not to rule them with political power, but to speak God's word that would shape their destiny.

His mission had two sides: to uproot and tear down, to destroy and overthrow — but also to build and to plant. Before God could build something new, the old corruption had to be removed.

This is a difficult truth: sometimes God must tear down before He can build up. Sometimes the most loving thing is honest confrontation. Jeremiah would spend most of his life delivering the hard message of uprooting — but the promise of planting was always there, waiting beyond the judgment.

The young prophet took his first steps into a ministry that would last over forty years.

ናይ ኣምላኽ ጻውዒት ንኤርምያስ ካብ ካልእ ነቢይ ዝተፈለየ ነበረ። ኣብ ልዕሊ ህዝብታትን መንግስትታትን ተሸየመ — ብፖለቲካዊ ሓይሊ ክገዝኦም ዘይኮነ ግን ቃል ኣምላኽ ክዛረብ።

ተልእኾኡ ክልተ ገጽ ነበሮ፡ ክምሕውን ክፈርስን — ግን ከምኡ'ውን ክሃንጽን ክተክልን። ቅድሚ ኣምላኽ ሓድሽ ነገር ምህናጹ ናይ ቀደም ብልሽውና ክእለ ነበሮ።

እቲ ንእሽቶ ነቢይ ናብ ልዕሊ ኣርብዓ ዓመት ዝጸንሐ ኣገልግሎት ቀዳማይ ስጉምቲ ወሰደ።

(Nay Amlak tsaw'it ni'Ermyas kab kal'e nebiy zitefelye nebere. Ab li'li hizbitatn mengstitatn tesheyeme — bipolotikawy hayli kigez'om zeyikone gin qal Amlak kizareb.)
Scene 6
[PICTURE: Young Jeremiah preaching passionately in a marketplace, gesturing toward idols on a hilltop while people go about their business, some glancing at him. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

Jeremiah began his prophetic ministry during the reign of good King Josiah. He preached in the streets of Jerusalem, calling the people of Judah to return to the living God.

"Your ancestors abandoned me," God spoke through Jeremiah. "They followed worthless idols and became worthless themselves. They exchanged their glorious God for gods that are not gods at all."

The people had placed idols on every high hill and under every spreading tree. They burned incense to Baal and poured out offerings to foreign gods. Yet they still came to the temple, thinking they could worship both God and idols.

Jeremiah pleaded: "Return, faithless people! I will cure you of your backsliding." But his words fell like seeds on hard ground.

ኤርምያስ ኣብ ግዜ ጽቡቕ ንጉስ ዮስያስ ናይ ነቢይ ኣገልግሎቱ ጀመረ። ኣብ ጐደናታት ኢየሩሳሌም ሰቢኹ ንህዝቢ ይሁዳ ናብ ህያው ኣምላኽ ክምለሱ ጸውዐ።

"ኣቦታትኩም ገዲፎምኒ" ኣምላኽ ብኤርምያስ ተዛረበ። "ንኸንቱ ጣኦታት ሰዓቡ ንሳቶም'ውን ከንቱ ኮኑ። ንኽቡር ኣምላኾም ብዘይኮኑ ኣማልኽቲ ቀየርዎ።"

ኤርምያስ ለመነ፡ "ተመለሱ ዘይእሙናት ህዝቢ!" ግን ቃላቱ ከም ዘርኢ ኣብ ጽኑዕ መሬት ወደቐ።

(Ermyas ab gize tsibuq nigus Yosyas nay nebiy agelgilotu jemere. Ab godenatat Iyerusalem sebiku ni'hizbi Yihuda nab hiyal Amlak kimilesu tsew'e.)
Scene 7
[PICTURE: An angry crowd of people turning their backs on Jeremiah, some shaking fists, others covering their ears. Jeremiah stands alone with a look of sorrowful determination. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

The people's response was swift and brutal: "We will not listen!" They hardened their hearts against Jeremiah's message. The priests mocked him. The false prophets contradicted him. The people preferred comfortable lies to uncomfortable truth.

"Peace, peace," the false prophets proclaimed, "everything is fine!" But Jeremiah knew there was no peace — judgment was coming as surely as winter follows autumn.

Even his own family in Anathoth turned against him. "Do not prophesy in the name of the LORD," they threatened, "or you will die by our hands." His own brothers and relatives plotted against him.

This was only the beginning of rejection that would last his entire lifetime. Jeremiah learned early that faithfulness to God does not guarantee popularity with people.

መልሲ ህዝቢ ቅልጡፍን ጨካንን ነበረ፡ "ኣይንሰምዕን!" ልቦም ኣብ ልዕሊ መልእኽቲ ኤርምያስ ኣትረሩ። ካህናት ኣላገጹሉ። ናይ ሓሶት ነቢያት ተቓወምዎ።

"ሰላም ሰላም" ናይ ሓሶት ነቢያት ኣወጁ "ኹሉ ጽቡቕ እዩ!" ግን ኤርምያስ ሰላም ከም ዘየለ ይፈልጥ ነበረ።

ናይ ገዛእ ርእሱ ስድራ ቤት'ውን ኣብ ዓናቶት ተጻረርዎ። "ብስም እግዚኣብሄር ኣይትነበ" ኢሎም ኣፈራርሕዎ።

(Melsi hizbi qiltufin chekanin nebere: "Ayniism'in!" Libom ab li'li mel'ikhti Ermyas atreru. Kahnat alagetsulu.)
Scene 8
[PICTURE: Jeremiah standing boldly at the gates of the great temple in Jerusalem, pointing inside while priests and worshippers stare in shock. The temple is grand with pillars. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

God sent Jeremiah to stand at the gate of the temple itself — the holiest site in all of Judah. There, he delivered the most controversial sermon of his life.

"Do not trust in deceptive words and say, 'This is the temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD!'" Jeremiah thundered. The people believed that as long as the temple stood, God would never let Jerusalem fall. They used religion as a security blanket while living in sin.

"Will you steal and murder, commit adultery and perjury, burn incense to Baal — and then come and stand before me in this house and say, 'We are safe'?" God demanded through His prophet.

Jeremiah warned that God would destroy the temple just as He had destroyed Shiloh centuries before. The people were furious. They nearly killed him that day.

ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ ኣብ ደገ ቤተ መቕደስ ክቐውም ለኣኾ — እቲ ዝቐደሰ ቦታ ኣብ ኹላ ይሁዳ። ኣብኡ እቲ ዝያዳ ዘከራኸረ ስብከት ኣቕረበ።

"ብሓሳዊ ቃላት ኣይትእመኑ 'ቤተ መቕደስ እግዚኣብሄር ቤተ መቕደስ እግዚኣብሄር' ኢልኩም!" ኤርምያስ ነጐደ። ህዝቢ ቤተ መቕደስ ክሳብ ዘሎ ኢየሩሳሌም ከም ዘይትወድቕ ይኣምኑ ነበሩ።

ኤርምያስ ኣምላኽ ንቤተ መቕደስ ከም ንሺሎ ከም ዘጥፍኣ ኣጠንቀቐ። ህዝቢ ተቘጥዑ። በታ መዓልቲ ክቐትልዎ ዳርጋ ነበሩ።

(Amlak ni'Ermyas ab dege bete meqdes kiqewm le'akho — iti ziqedese bota ab kula Yihuda. Abu iti ziyada zikerakhere sibket aqrebe.)
Scene 9
[PICTURE: Jeremiah walking alone through the streets of Jerusalem, watching families celebrate together while he remains solitary. His face shows acceptance mixed with loneliness. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

God gave Jeremiah an unusual command: "You must not marry and have sons or daughters in this place." This was extraordinary — in ancient Israel, marriage and children were considered the greatest blessings.

But Jeremiah's solitary life was itself a prophecy. God explained: "The sons and daughters born in this land will die of deadly diseases. They will not be mourned or buried." Jeremiah's loneliness was a living warning of the catastrophe to come.

God also told him not to enter houses of mourning or feasting. Jeremiah could not grieve with the grieving or celebrate with the celebrating. His entire existence became a message.

Imagine carrying this weight — watching others build families while you remain alone, knowing it is God's will for your life. Jeremiah obeyed, though it cost him deeply.

ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ ዘይልሙድ ትእዛዝ ሃቦ፡ "ኣብዚ ቦታ ኣይትመርዓ ኣወዳትን ኣዋልድን ኣይትውለድ።" እዚ ፍሉይ ነበረ — ኣብ ጥንታዊት እስራኤል ሓዳርን ውሉድን ዝዓበየ በረኸት ነበረ።

ግን ናይ ኤርምያስ በይናዊ ሂወት ባዕሉ ትንቢት ነበረ። ንኤርምያስ በይንነቱ ናይ ዝመጽእ ዕንወት ህያው ምጥንቃቕ ነበረ።

ኣምላኽ ኣብ ቤት ሓዘን ወይ ባህታ ከይኣቱ ነገሮ። ምሉእ ህላወኡ መልእኽቲ ኮነ።

(Amlak ni'Ermyas zeyilmud ti'izaz habo: "Abzi bota ayitmer'a awedatn awaldn aytitwled." Izi filuy nebere — ab tintawit Israel hadarn wiludn zi'abeye bereket nebere.)
Scene 10
[PICTURE: Jeremiah watching a potter at his wheel, the potter's hands reshaping a spoiled clay vessel into something new. The potter concentrates intently on his work. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

God told Jeremiah, "Go down to the potter's house, and there I will give you my message." So Jeremiah went and watched the potter working at his wheel.

The pot the potter was shaping was marred in his hands. But instead of throwing the clay away, the potter reshaped it into another pot, forming it as seemed best to him.

Then God's word came: "Can I not do with you, Israel, as this potter does? Like clay in the hand of the potter, so are you in my hand." God was saying: I have the right to reshape nations. If a nation turns from evil, I will relent from judgment. If it turns to evil, I will reconsider the good I intended.

This was both a warning and a hope. The clay was marred — but the Potter had not given up. He was still willing to reshape, if only the people would yield to His hands.

ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ "ናብ ቤት ሰራሕ ስራሕ ውረድ ኣብኡ መልእኽተይ ክህበካ እየ" በሎ። ኤርምያስ ከይዱ ንሰራሕ ስራሕ ኣብ መዐወሪኡ ክሰርሕ ተዓዘበ።

እቲ ዝስራሕ ዝነበረ ድስቲ ኣብ ኢዱ ተበላሸወ። ግን ነቲ ጭቃ ኣይደርበዮን — ናብ ካልእ ድስቲ ቀየሮ ከም ዝደለዮ መልክዕ ሃቦ።

ሽዑ ቃል ኣምላኽ መጸ፡ "ከምዚ ሰራሕ ስራሕ ክገብረኩም ኣይክእልንዶ እስራኤል? ከም ጭቃ ኣብ ኢድ ሰራሕ ስራሕ ከምኡ ኢኹም ኣብ ኢደይ።"

(Amlak ni'Ermyas "Nab bet serah sirah wured abu mel'ikhtey kihbeka iye" belo. Ermyas keydu ni'serah sirah ab me'eweriu kisirih te'azebe.)

Throughout his ministry, Jeremiah did something remarkable — he recorded his private conversations with God. These "confessions" or laments reveal a man who was brutally honest with his Creator.

"Why does the way of the wicked prosper? Why do all the faithless live at ease?" he demanded. "You planted them, and they have taken root; they grow and bear fruit. You are always on their lips but far from their hearts."

"Cursed be the day I was born!" he cried in his darkest moments. "Why did I ever come out of the womb to see trouble and sorrow and to end my days in shame?"

These prayers teach us something vital: God can handle our honesty. He prefers raw, real prayer over polished, false praise. Jeremiah brought his anger, confusion, and despair directly to God — and God never condemned him for it.

ኣብ ምሉእ ኣገልግሎቱ ኤርምያስ ዘደንቕ ነገር ገበረ — ምስ ኣምላኽ ዝገበሮ ብሕታዊ ዝርርብ ጸሓፈ። እዘን "ናይ ኑዛዜ ጸሎታት" ምስ ፈጣሪኡ ብሓቂ ቅኑዕ ዝነበረ ሰብ የርእያና።

"ስለምንታይ መገዲ ረሲኣን ይሰልጥ? ስለምንታይ ዘይኣምኑ ብዘይ ጭንቀት ይነብሩ?" ኢሉ ሓተተ።

"ርግምቲ ይውረድ ኣብታ ዝተወለድኩላ መዓልቲ!" ኣብ ዝጸልመተ እዋኑ ኣእወየ።

እዘን ጸሎታት ኣገዳሲ ነገር ይምህራና፡ ኣምላኽ ቅንዕናና ክቕበል ይኽእል እዩ።

(Ab milu'e agelgilotu Ermyas zidenqi neger gebere — mis Amlak zigibero bihtawy zirirb tsehafe. Izen "nay nuzaze tselotot" mis fetariu bihaqqi qinu'u zinebere seb yer'iyana.)
Scene 12
[PICTURE: Jeremiah standing in a field, looking up at the sky with arms outstretched in frustration and surrender, tears on his cheeks but determination in his posture. Storm clouds and sunlight mix above. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

"You deceived me, LORD, and I was deceived," Jeremiah cried out. "You overpowered me and prevailed. I am ridiculed all day long; everyone mocks me."

These shocking words reveal a prophet wrestling with God — not abandoning Him, but confronting Him. Jeremiah felt tricked. God had promised to be with him, yet the suffering never stopped. The rejection never ended. The loneliness only grew deeper.

Have you ever felt that way? That God led you somewhere difficult and then seemed to disappear? Jeremiah teaches us that this wrestling is part of faith, not the absence of it. He questioned God — but he never walked away.

Even in his accusation, Jeremiah could not deny what he knew: God was real, God had called him, and despite everything, God was still there.

"ኣታሊልካኒ እግዚኣብሄር ተታሊለ ድማ" ኤርምያስ ጸውዐ። "ሓይልካ ኣርኣየካኒ ተዓዊትካ ድማ። ምሉእ መዓልቲ ይላገጹለይ ኩሉ ይንዕቐኒ።"

እዘን ዘስድዓ ቃላት ምስ ኣምላኽ ዝቃለስ ነቢይ የርእያና — ዘይገድፎ ግን ዝገጥሞ። ኤርምያስ ተታሊሉ ተሰማዖ። ግን ካብ ኣምላኽ ኣይከደን።

እዚ ምቅላስ ናይ እምነት ክፋል እዩ እምበር ናይ ምጥፋእ ኣይኮነን። ንኣምላኽ ሓተተ — ግን ፈጺሙ ኣይገደፎን።

(Atalilkani Egzi'abher tetaliledima Ermyas tsew'e. "Haylka ar'ayekani te'awitka dima. Milu'e me'alti yilagetsuley kulu yini'qeni.")
Scene 13
[PICTURE: Jeremiah with his hand over his chest, fire glowing within him like burning embers visible through his clothing, unable to contain the urgency of God's message. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

Despite all his suffering, Jeremiah discovered he could not stop speaking God's word. He tried. Oh, how he tried!

"But if I say, 'I will not mention His word or speak anymore in His name,' His word is in my heart like a fire, a fire shut up in my bones. I am weary of holding it in; indeed, I cannot."

This is one of the most powerful descriptions of prophetic calling in all of Scripture. Jeremiah wanted to quit — the pain was too great, the rejection too constant. But God's word burned inside him like a fire that could not be contained.

Sometimes obedience is not joyful enthusiasm. Sometimes it is simply being unable to do otherwise because God's truth has become part of who you are. Jeremiah could no more stop prophesying than he could stop breathing.

ብዘይ ኩሉ ስቓዩ ኤርምያስ ቃል ኣምላኽ ክዛረብ ከም ዘይክእል ደረሰ። ፈቲኑ። ኦ ክንደይ ፈቲኑ!

"ግን ቃሉ ኣይጠቅስን ብስሙ ድማ ኣይዛረብን እንተ በልኩ ቃሉ ኣብ ልበይ ከም ሓዊ ኮነ፡ ኣብ ኣዕጽምተይ ተዓሺጉ ዘሎ ሓዊ። ክሕዞ ደኺመ ክሕዞ ኣይክእልን ድማ።"

እዚ ኣብ ኩሉ ቅዱስ ጽሑፍ ካብ ዝሓየለ ገለጻ ናይ ነቢያዊ ጻውዒት እዩ። ኤርምያስ ከቋርጽ ደለየ — ግን ቃል ኣምላኽ ኣብ ውሽጢ ከም ዘይትዓቐብ ሓዊ ነደደ።

God told Jeremiah to write all His prophecies on a scroll. Jeremiah dictated every word to his faithful scribe, Baruch, who carefully recorded the messages on a long parchment.

The scroll was read publicly, and word reached King Jehoiakim. The king commanded that it be read to him. It was winter, and the king sat by a fire in his palace.

As each section was read, the king took a scribe's knife, cut off the portion that had been read, and threw it into the fire. Column by column, he burned God's word. No fear. No repentance. Just arrogant contempt.

The courtiers who watched did not tear their clothes in grief. They showed no fear. They had become so hardened that the word of God meant nothing to them. But truth cannot be destroyed by fire — as the king would soon discover.

ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ ኩሉ ትንቢታቱ ኣብ ጥቕለል ክጽሕፍ ነገሮ። ኤርምያስ ንኣሚን ጸሓፊኡ ባሩክ ቃል ብቓል ኣንበቦ።

እቲ ጥቕለል ብግሉጽ ተነብበ ዜና ድማ ናብ ንጉስ ዮኣቂም በጸሐ። ንጉስ ኣብ ጥቓ ሓዊ ኮፍ ኢሉ ከንብብዎ ኣዘዘ።

ነፍሲ ወከፍ ክፋል ምስ ተነብበ ንጉስ ብቤላ ቆሪጹ ናብ ሓዊ ደርበዮ። ዓምዲ ብዓምዲ ቃል ኣምላኽ ኣቃጸለ። ፍርሒ የለን። ንስሓ የለን። ትዕቢት ንዕቀት ጥራይ።

(Amlak ni'Ermyas kulu tinbitattu ab tiqlelel kitsihif negero. Ermyas ni'amin tsehafiyu Baruk qal biqal anbebo.)
Scene 15
[PICTURE: Jeremiah and Baruch working together by lamplight, Baruch writing intently on a new scroll while Jeremiah dictates with quiet determination. A burned scroll's ashes visible nearby. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

After the king burned the scroll, God spoke to Jeremiah: "Take another scroll and write on it all the words that were on the first scroll." So Jeremiah dictated again to Baruch — every prophecy, every warning, every word the king had tried to destroy.

But this time, God added even more words of judgment against the king who had dared to burn His message. Jehoiakim's contempt did not silence God — it only increased the judgment upon himself.

This is a powerful lesson: you cannot destroy truth by refusing to hear it. You cannot escape God's word by burning the page it's written on. Truth is not fragile. It survives every fire, every persecution, every attempt to silence it.

Jeremiah and Baruch simply wrote it all again — with additions. Persistence in the face of opposition is one of faith's greatest weapons.

ንጉስ ነቲ ጥቕለል ድሕሪ ምቅጻሉ ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ ተዛረቦ፡ "ካልእ ጥቕለል ውሰድ ኣብቲ ቀዳማይ ጥቕለል ዝነበረ ኹሉ ቃላት ድማ ጽሓፈሉ።"

ኤርምያስ ከም ብሓድሽ ንባሩክ ኣንበቦ — ነፍሲ ወከፍ ትንቢት ነፍሲ ወከፍ ምጥንቃቕ ነፍሲ ወከፍ ቃል ንጉስ ከጥፍኦ ዝፈተነ። ግን ሕጂ ኣምላኽ ኣብ ልዕሊ ንጉስ ተወሳኺ ፍርዲ ወሰኸ።

ሓቂ ብምስማዕ ምእባይ ኣይትጥፍኣን። ጽሑፍ ብምቅጻል ካብ ቃል ኣምላኽ ኣይትሃድምን። ሓቂ ስብራቲ ኣይኮነን።

(Nigus neti tiqlelel diHri miqtsalu Amlak ni'Ermyas tezarebo: "Kal'e tiqlelel wused abti qedamay tiqlelel zinebere kulu qalat dima tsihafelu.")

During the final siege of Jerusalem, Jeremiah continued to preach: "Surrender to Babylon and live. Stay and fight, and you will die." The officials were furious. "This man is weakening the soldiers!" they told King Zedekiah. "He must be put to death!"

The weak king gave in: "He is in your hands." So they took Jeremiah and lowered him by ropes into a cistern — a deep, underground water storage pit. There was no water in it, but there was thick mud at the bottom.

Jeremiah sank into the mud. Alone in the darkness. Left to die slowly of starvation and cold. This was the reward for forty years of faithful service — abandoned in a pit because he told the truth.

How many times had he warned them? How many tears had he shed? And this was their answer: silence him in the mud.

ኣብ ናይ መወዳእታ ክባብ ኢየሩሳሌም ኤርምያስ ክሰብኽ ቀጸለ፡ "ንባቢሎን ተወፈዩ ብህይወት ትነብሩ። ጸኒሕኩም ተዋጊኡ ትሞቱ።" ሓለቃታት ተቘጥዑ።

ድኹም ንጉስ ተቐበለ፡ "ኣብ ኢድኩም እዩ።" ንኤርምያስ ብገመድ ናብ ጉድጓድ ኣውረድዎ — ዓሚቝ ናይ ማይ ማዕቆብ። ማይ ኣይነበሮን ግን ሓመድ ነበሮ።

Scene 17
[PICTURE: An Ethiopian/Cushite court official named Ebed-Melech directing servants to lower old rags and ropes into the cistern to rescue Jeremiah. Jeremiah reaches up gratefully from the mud below. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

But God had not forgotten His prophet. In the royal court there was a man named Ebed-Melech, a Cushite — an African — who served as an official in the palace. When he heard what had been done to Jeremiah, he went directly to King Zedekiah.

"My lord the king, these men have acted wickedly. They have thrown Jeremiah into the cistern, where he will starve to death." The king authorized the rescue: "Take thirty men and lift Jeremiah out before he dies."

Ebed-Melech showed remarkable kindness. He gathered old rags and worn-out clothes, lowered them to Jeremiah, and said, "Put these under your arms to pad the ropes." He cared not just about saving Jeremiah's life, but about not hurting him further.

God always provides unexpected allies. Sometimes help comes from the person you least expect — a foreigner, an outsider, someone with nothing to gain but a clear conscience.

ግን ኣምላኽ ንነቢዩ ኣይረስዖን። ኣብ ቤተ መንግስቲ ዓብድ-መለክ ዝስሙ ሰብ ነበረ፡ ኩሻዊ — ኣፍሪቃዊ — ኣብ ቤተ መንግስቲ ዘገልግል። ንኤርምያስ ዝተገብረ ምስ ሰምዐ ብቐጥታ ናብ ንጉስ ጺድቅያስ ከደ።

"ጐይታይ ንጉስ እዞም ሰባት ብእኩይ ገይሮም። ንኤርምያስ ኣብ ጉድጓድ ደርብዮምዎ ብጥሜት ክመውት እዩ።" ንጉስ ናይ ምድሓን ፍቓድ ሃበ።

(Gin Amlak ni'nebiyu ayres'on. Ab bete mengisti Abed-Melek zisimu seb nebere, Kushawi — Afriqawi. Ni'Ermyas zitegebre mis sem'e biqetita nab nigus Tsidiqiyas kede.)
Scene 18

In 586 BC, after years of warnings that went unheeded, everything Jeremiah had prophesied came true. Nebuchadnezzar's army breached the walls of Jerusalem. The city that had seemed invincible fell.

The temple — the one they believed God would never allow to be destroyed — was burned to the ground. The palace was destroyed. The walls were torn down. The people were killed or carried away to Babylon in chains.

King Zedekiah tried to flee but was captured. His sons were killed before his eyes, and then his eyes were put out — the last thing he ever saw was his children's death. He was led to Babylon in chains.

Jeremiah had been right all along. For over forty years he had warned them. But being right brought him no joy — only the deepest sorrow imaginable.

ኣብ 586 ቅ.ል.ክ ድሕሪ ዓመታት ዘይተሰምዐ ምጥንቃቕ ኩሉ ኤርምያስ ዝተነበዮ ሓቂ ኮነ። ሰራዊት ናቡከደነጸር ንመንደቕ ኢየሩሳሌም ሰበሮ። እታ ዘይትስዓር ትመስል ዝነበረት ከተማ ወደቐት።

ቤተ መቕደስ — እቲ ኣምላኽ ፈጺሙ ከም ዘየፍርሶ ዝኣመኑ — ብሓዊ ነደደ። ቤተ መንግስቲ ፈረሰ። መናድቕ ፈረሰ። ህዝቢ ተቐትለ ወይ ናብ ባቢሎን ብሰንሰለት ተወስደ።

ኤርምያስ ኩሉ ግዜ ቅኑዕ ነበረ። ንልዕሊ ኣርብዓ ዓመት ኣጠንቂቑዎም። ግን ቅኑዕ ምዃን ሓጐስ ኣየምጽኣሉን — ዝዓሚቘ ሓዘን ጥራይ።

Jeremiah did not celebrate being proven right. He wept. He is called "the weeping prophet" because his response to judgment was not "I told you so" but overwhelming grief.

"Oh, that my head were a spring of water and my eyes a fountain of tears! I would weep day and night for the slain of my people," he had written earlier. Now that grief became reality.

The book of Lamentations, traditionally attributed to Jeremiah, captures his agony: "How deserted lies the city, once so full of people! How like a widow is she, who once was great among the nations!"

This teaches us something profound about prophetic love: Jeremiah did not warn them because he wanted to be right. He warned them because he loved them. And when they suffered the consequences of their rebellion, he suffered with them. True love grieves even when it has been rejected.

ኤርምያስ ቅኑዕ ምዃኑ ስለ ዝተረጋገጸ ኣየኽበረን። በኸየ። "ብኽያም ነቢይ" ይብሃል ምኽንያቱ ንፍርዲ ዝሃቦ መልሲ "ነጊረኩም ነይረ" ዘይኮነ ዝዓርፍ ዘይብሉ ሓዘን ነበረ።

"ርእሰይ ናይ ማይ ዓይኒ ኣዒንተይ ከኣ ናይ ንብዓት ፍልፍል እንተ ዝኸውን! ንቕቱላት ህዝበይ ለይትን ቀትርን ምበኸኹ" ቀደም ጽሒፉ ነበረ።

ናይ ሰቆቃው መጽሓፍ ስቓዩ ይገልጽ፡ "ክንደይ ጥራያ ኮይና እታ ብህዝቢ ትመልእ ዝነበረት ከተማ!"

እዚ ዓሚቝ ትምህርቲ ናይ ነቢያዊ ፍቕሪ ይህበና፡ ኤርምያስ ቅኑዕ ክኸውን ስለ ዝደለየ ኣይኮነን ዘጠንቀቐ። ስለ ዘፍቀሮም እዩ።

But in the midst of all this darkness, Jeremiah delivered one of the most hopeful prophecies in all of Scripture — the promise of a New Covenant:

"The days are coming," declares the LORD, "when I will make a new covenant with the people of Israel. It will not be like the covenant I made with their ancestors... I will put my law in their minds and write it on their hearts. I will be their God, and they will be my people."

"No longer will they teach their neighbor, saying, 'Know the LORD,' because they will all know me, from the least of them to the greatest. For I will forgive their wickedness and will remember their sins no more." (Jeremiah 31:31-34)

This prophecy points directly to Jesus and the gift of the Holy Spirit. God's law would no longer be external rules on stone — it would become an internal reality written on the heart. This is the covenant we live under today.

ኣብ ማእከል እዚ ኹሉ ጸልማት ኤርምያስ ኣብ ኹሉ ቅዱስ ጽሑፍ ካብ ዝያዳ ዝተስፋ ዝህብ ትንቢት ኣቕረበ — ናይ ሓድሽ ኪዳን ተስፋ፡

"መዓልታት ይመጻ" ይብል እግዚኣብሄር "ምስ ህዝቢ እስራኤል ሓድሽ ኪዳን ዝኣትወላ። ከምቲ ምስ ኣቦታቶም ዝኣተኹዎ ኣይኮነን... ሕገይ ኣብ ኣእምሮኦም ከንብሮ ኣብ ልቦም ከኣ ክጽሕፎ እየ። ኣምላኾም ክኸውን እየ ንሳቶም ድማ ህዝበይ ክኾኑ እዮም።"

"ድሕሪ ደጊም ንጎረቤቶም 'ንእግዚኣብሄር ፍለጥ' ኢሎም ኣይክምህሩን ኩሎም ካብ ንእሽቶ ክሳብ ዓቢ ክፈልጡኒ እዮም። ክፍኣቶም ክሓድገሎም ሓጢኣቶም ድማ ድሕሪ ደጊም ኣይክዝክሮን እየ።"

እዚ ትንቢት ብቐጥታ ናብ የሱስን ህያብ መንፈስ ቅዱስን ይእምት።

Even after Jerusalem fell, Jeremiah continued his ministry. He sent a letter to those already in exile in Babylon with one of the most beloved verses in Scripture:

"For I know the plans I have for you," declares the LORD, "plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future. Then you will call on me and come and pray to me, and I will listen to you. You will seek me and find me when you seek me with all your heart." (Jeremiah 29:11-13)

But this promise came with a surprising instruction: "Build houses and settle down; plant gardens and eat what they produce. Marry and have sons and daughters... Seek the peace and prosperity of the city where I have carried you into exile."

God was saying: this exile is not the end. I have a future for you. But right now, bloom where you are planted. Trust my timing, not your own.

ኢየሩሳሌም ድሕሪ ምውዳቓ'ውን ኤርምያስ ኣገልግሎቱ ቀጸለ። ድሮ ኣብ ባቢሎን ናብ ዝነበሩ ስደተኛታት ደብዳበ ለኣኸ ኣብ ቅዱስ ጽሑፍ ካብ ዝፍቀሩ ጥቕስታት ሓደ ዝሓዘ፡

"ብዛዕባኹም ዘለኒ ሓሳብ እፈልጦ እየ" ይብል እግዚኣብሄር "ናይ ሰላም ሓሳብ እምበር ናይ ክፉእ ኣይኮነን፡ ተስፋን መጻእን ክህበኩም። ሽዑ ክትጽውዑኒ ክትመጹ ክትጽልዩ ኢኹም ኣነ ድማ ክሰምዓኩም እየ።" (ኤርምያስ 29፡11-13)

ኣምላኽ ይብል ነበረ፡ እዚ ስደት መወዳእታ ኣይኮነን። መጻኢ ኣለኩም። ግን ሕጂ ኣብ ዘለኹሞ ቦታ ዕምበቡ። ንናተይ ግዜ ተኣመኑ።

(Iyerusalem diHri miwdaqa'win Ermyas agelgilotu qetsele. Dro ab Babilon nab zineberu sideteñatat debdabe le'akhe ab qidus tsihuf kab zifiqeru tiqistat hade zihaze.)
Scene 22
[PICTURE: An older Jeremiah, weathered but dignified, standing against a timeline showing decades of his ministry - from youth to old age. Seasons change behind him but his posture of faithfulness remains constant. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]

For over forty years, Jeremiah faithfully delivered God's message. Think about that: more than four decades of constant rejection. He served under five different kings, and not one of them truly listened.

He never saw revival in his lifetime. He never experienced the joy of a nation turning back to God. He never received honor or recognition from his people. He died in obscurity, likely in Egypt, taken there against his will by fleeing refugees.

By every worldly measure, Jeremiah's ministry was a failure. No converts. No followers. No victory parade. Just faithfulness — day after day, year after year, decade after decade.

But by God's measure, Jeremiah was one of the greatest prophets who ever lived. His words are still read today, three thousand years later. His prophecy of the New Covenant became the foundation of Christian faith. Faithfulness is never wasted, even when we cannot see the results.

ንልዕሊ ኣርብዓ ዓመት ኤርምያስ ብኣሚንነት መልእኽቲ ኣምላኽ ኣመሓላለፈ። ሕሰብዎ፡ ልዕሊ ኣርባዕተ ዓሰርተ ዓመት ናይ ቀጻሊ ምንጻግ። ኣብ ትሕቲ ሓሙሽተ ዝተፈላለዩ ነገስታት ኣገልገለ ሓደ'ውን ብሓቂ ኣይሰምዖን።

ኣብ ህይወቱ መንፈሳዊ ምሕዳስ ኣይረኣየን። ህዝቢ ናብ ኣምላኽ ክምለስ ናይ ሓጐስ ተመክሮ ኣይነበሮን። ክብሪ ወይ ኣፍልጦ ካብ ህዝቡ ኣይተቐበለን።

Scene 23

What did faithfulness cost Jeremiah? Everything. He never married or had children. He was rejected by his family, his community, his nation. He was beaten, imprisoned, thrown into a pit, and threatened with death countless times.

He lived in poverty while false prophets lived in luxury. He preached doom while they promised peace. The people loved them and hated him.

But what did faithfulness gain him? The privilege of carrying God's word. The honor of speaking truth when lies dominated. The eternal legacy of the New Covenant prophecy. And most importantly — the approval of God Himself.

Jesus later said, "Well done, good and faithful servant." Jeremiah may never have heard those words in this life. But he lived for an audience of One. And that One never forgot his faithfulness.

ኣሚንነት ንኤርምያስ እንታይ ወጻኦ? ኩሉ ነገር። ኣይተመርዓን ቆልዑ ኣይነበርዎን። ብስድራ ቤቱ ብማሕበረ ሰቡ ብሃገሩ ተነጸገ። ተወቕዐ ተኣስረ ኣብ ጉድጓድ ተደርበየ።

ናይ ሓሶት ነቢያት ብጥሪት ክነብሩ ከለዉ ንሱ ብድኽነት ነበረ። ጥፍኣት ክሰብኽ ከሎ ንሳቶም ሰላም ተስፋ ይህቡ ነበሩ።

ግን ኣሚንነት እንታይ ኣትረፎ? ቃል ኣምላኽ ናይ ምሓዝ መሰል። ሓሶት ክዓብል ከሎ ሓቂ ናይ ምዝራብ ክብሪ። ናይ ሓድሽ ኪዳን ትንቢት ዘልኣለማዊ ውርሻ። ቀንዲ ድማ — ናይ ኣምላኽ ባዕሉ ፍቓድ።

Jeremiah foreshadowed Jesus in remarkable ways. Both were rejected prophets who loved their people despite being hated. Both wept over Jerusalem. Both were persecuted by religious leaders. Both spoke truth that people refused to hear.

Jesus Himself was sometimes mistaken for Jeremiah (Matthew 16:14). The people saw the same spirit in Jesus — the weeping prophet who loved fiercely and spoke truth fearlessly.

But where Jeremiah could only prophesy the New Covenant, Jesus established it. Where Jeremiah's tears could not save, Jesus' blood could. Where Jeremiah's words pointed to hope, Jesus became the hope.

Jeremiah's life was a shadow pointing toward a greater Prophet who would come — one who would not just announce God's forgiveness but accomplish it through His own sacrifice on the cross.

ኤርምያስ ንየሱስ ብዘደንቕ መንገዲ ኣመልከተ። ክልቲኦም ተነጺጎም ንህዝቦም ዘፍቀሩ ነቢያት ነበሩ። ክልቲኦም ንኢየሩሳሌም በኸዩ። ክልቲኦም ብሃይማኖታዊ መራሕቲ ተሰጉ።

የሱስ ባዕሉ ሓደ ሓደ ግዜ ምስ ኤርምያስ ይደናገር ነበረ (ማቴዎስ 16፡14)። ህዝቢ ኣብ የሱስ ተመሳሳሊ መንፈስ ረኣዩ።

ግን ኤርምያስ ሓድሽ ኪዳን ክንበ ጥራይ ከሎ የሱስ ኣቖሞ። ንብዓት ኤርምያስ ከድሕን ዘይክኣለ ደም የሱስ ኣድሓነ። ቃላት ኤርምያስ ናብ ተስፋ ዝእምት ከሎ የሱስ ባዕሉ ተስፋ ኮነ።

What does Jeremiah's story mean for you today? Perhaps you feel too young to make a difference. Perhaps you've spoken truth and been rejected. Perhaps you wonder if faithfulness matters when no one seems to notice.

Jeremiah's life answers: God knew you before you were born. Your age does not limit your calling. Faithfulness is measured by obedience, not by results. And God's plans for you are good — even when the path is painful.

You may never see the full fruit of your faithfulness in this life. Jeremiah didn't. But every act of obedience matters eternally. Every truth spoken in love echoes through generations. Every prayer of honest struggle reaches God's heart.

Like Jeremiah, you are called to be faithful — not successful by the world's standards, but faithful by God's. Trust that the One who formed you in the womb has purposes for you that extend far beyond what you can see today.

ህይወት ኤርምያስ ይምልስ፡ ኣምላኽ ቅድሚ ምውላድካ ይፈልጠካ ነይሩ። ዕድሜኻ ጻውዒትካ ኣይድርቶን። ኣሚንነት ብኣይምሮት ይዕቀን እምበር ብውጽኢት ኣይኮነን።

ከም ኤርምያስ ኣሚን ክትከውን ኢኻ ዝተጸዋዕካ — ብዓለማዊ መዐቀኒ ዕዉት ዘይኮነ ግን ብመዐቀኒ ኣምላኽ ኣሚን። እቲ ኣብ ማህጸን ዝፈጠረካ ካብ ሎሚ ትርእዮ ንላዕሊ ዝሓልፍ ዕላማ ከም ዘለዎ ተኣመን።

The Moral of the Story

Faithfulness is not measured by results — it is measured by obedience.

Jeremiah's life teaches us that God calls us before we are born and equips us for purposes we may never fully understand. Being young is no barrier to God's calling. Being rejected is not a sign of failure. Speaking truth when it is unpopular is one of the bravest things a person can do.

Like fire shut up in our bones, God's truth demands to be spoken. We may not see the harvest from our faithfulness, but God sees every seed planted, every tear shed, and every prayer offered. His plans for us are good — plans for hope and a future — even when the present is painful.

The question is not "Will people listen?" but "Will I be faithful?" Jeremiah answered that question with his entire life: Yes, Lord. No matter the cost.

Discussion Questions

  1. Jeremiah said "I am too young" when God called him. Have you ever felt too young or unqualified for something God was asking you to do? How does God's response to Jeremiah encourage you?
    ኤርምያስ ኣምላኽ ክጽውዖ ከሎ "ንእሽቶ እየ" በለ። ኣምላኽ ክገብሮ ንዝሓተካ ነገር ንእሽቶ ወይ ብቑዕ ከም ዘይኮንካ ተሰሚዑካ ይፈልጥዶ? መልሲ ኣምላኽ ንኤርምያስ ብኸመይ የተባብዓካ?
  2. Jeremiah was faithful for over 40 years without seeing positive results. How do you stay faithful to God when you don't see immediate results from your obedience? What motivates you when no one seems to notice?
    ኤርምያስ ንልዕሊ 40 ዓመት ኣወንታዊ ውጽኢት ከይረኣየ ኣሚን ነበረ። ቅልጡፍ ውጽኢት ኣብ ዘይትርእየሉ ብኸመይ ንኣምላኽ ኣሚን ትኸውን? ዝሪኤካ ዘየለ ክመስል ከሎ እንታይ የበራትዓካ?
  3. Jeremiah prayed very honest, raw prayers — even accusing God of deceiving him. Do you think it's okay to be completely honest with God about your frustrations and doubts? Why or why not?
    ኤርምያስ ብጣዕሚ ቅኑዕ ጸሎታት ጸሊዩ — ንኣምላኽ'ውን ከም ዘታለሎ ከሲስዎ። ንኣምላኽ ብዛዕባ ጭንቀትካን ጥርጣረኻን ብምሉእ ቅንዕና ክትዛረቦ ቅኑዕ ዲዩ ኢልካ ትሓስብ? ስለምንታይ?
  4. Speaking truth when it's unpopular is very difficult. Can you think of a time when you had to say something true even though others didn't want to hear it? What happened? How can we speak truth with both courage and love?
    ተፈታዊ ኣብ ዘይኮነሉ ግዜ ሓቂ ምዝራብ ኣዝዩ ኣጸጋሚ እዩ። ካልኦት ክሰምዕዎ ዘይደልዩ ሓቂ ክትብል ዘድለየካ ግዜ ትዝክርዶ? እንታይ ኮነ? ብትብዓትን ፍቕርን ብኸመይ ሓቂ ንዛረብ?
  5. Jeremiah 29:11 says God has plans to give us "hope and a future." How do you hold onto this promise during times of suffering or confusion? How does Jeremiah's story help you trust God's long-term plan even when things seem to be falling apart?
    ኤርምያስ 29፡11 ኣምላኽ "ተስፋን መጻእን" ዝህብ መደብ ከም ዘለዎ ይብል። ኣብ ግዜ ስቓይ ወይ ምድንጋር ነዛ ተስፋ ብኸመይ ትሕዛ? ዛንታ ኤርምያስ ኩሉ ነገር ክፈርስ ክመስል ከሎ ንናይ ኣምላኽ ነዊሕ ግዜ መደብ ክትኣምን ብኸመይ ይሕግዘካ?

Let Us Pray

Dear Heavenly Father,

Thank You for knowing us before we were born and setting us apart for Your purposes. Thank You for the example of Jeremiah — a man who was faithful when it cost him everything.

Give us courage like Jeremiah to speak Your truth even when it is unpopular. Give us faithfulness regardless of results — help us to obey You whether people listen or not. Give us honest hearts that bring our real feelings to You in prayer, knowing You can handle our doubts and frustrations.

Lord, when we feel too young or too small for what You're calling us to do, remind us that You do not measure by age or ability but by willingness. When we face rejection for doing right, help us remember that Your approval matters more than human applause.

Write Your law on our hearts as You promised through Jeremiah. Make Your word like fire in our bones that we cannot contain. And help us trust Your plans for hope and a future, even when the present is painful and the path is unclear.

In Jesus' name we pray, Amen.
