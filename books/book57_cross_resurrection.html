Scene 1 of 25

The road into Jerusalem was alive with noise and color as Jesus rode in on a young donkey. Crowds pressed in from every side, tearing branches from palm trees and spreading their cloaks across the dusty path. "Hosanna! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord!" they shouted, their voices rising like thunder. They expected a conquering king who would overthrow Rome, but Jesus came in humility — on a donkey, not a warhorse. This was the entrance of a different kind of king, one whose kingdom would be established not through military power but through sacrificial love. The disciples felt the electricity in the air, yet none of them understood that this triumphal entry was the beginning of the most painful and glorious week in human history.
እቲ ናብ ኢየሩሳሌም ዝወስድ መንገዲ ብድምጽን ሕብርን ህያው ነበረ፣ የሱስ ኣብ ልዕሊ ንእሽተይ ኣድጊ ተቐሚጡ ክኣቱ ከሎ። ብዙሓት ሰባት ካብ ኩሉ ሸነኽ ተጸጊዖም፣ ካብ ተኽሊ ተምሪ ጨናፍር ቆሪጾም ክዳውንቶም ኣብቲ ዑፉን መንገዲ ይነፍሕዎ ነበሩ። "ሆሳእና! ብስም እግዚኣብሄር ዝመጽእ ብሩኽ ይኹን!" ኢሎም ጨዲሮም። ሓደ ዓይነት ንጉስ — ብትሕትና ዝመጽእ ንጉስ ነበረ። እዚ ኣእታዊ፣ ብመስዋእታዊ ፍቕሪ ዝቖመ ንግስነት ናይ ዝተፈለየ ንጉስ ምእታው ነበረ።

[PICTURE: Jesus standing on the Mount of Olives overlooking Jerusalem, tears streaming down his face. The beautiful city with its temple gleams below, but Jesus's expression shows deep sorrow. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
As the crowds celebrated around Him, Jesus paused on the Mount of Olives and looked out over Jerusalem — and wept. These were not quiet tears but deep sobs of grief for a city He loved with His whole being. He could see the magnificent temple, the bustling markets, the homes filled with families, and He knew what was coming — not just His own suffering, but the destruction that would fall upon this city because it refused to recognize the time of God's coming. "If only you had known what would bring you peace," He cried out. Here we see something profound about God's heart: even knowing the rejection and violence He was about to face, Jesus's first emotion was compassion — grief not for Himself, but for those who would suffer because they turned away from love itself.
እቶም ብዙሓት ኣብ ዙርያኡ ክበዓሉ ከለዉ፣ የሱስ ኣብ ደብረ ዘይቲ ደው ኢሉ ናብ ኢየሩሳሌም ጠሚቱ — ነቢዑ። እዚኦም ህድእ ዝበሉ ንብዓት ኣይነበሩን ግን ዓሚቝ ናይ ሓዘን ብኽያት ነበሩ ንሓንቲ ከተማ ብምሉእ ልቡ ዘፍቅራ። "ንሰላምኪ ዝጠቅም እንተ ትፈልጢ ጥራይ" ኢሉ ኣእዊዩ። ኣብዚ ዓሚቝ ነገር ብዛዕባ ልቢ ኣምላኽ ንርኢ ኢና — ነቲ ዝመጽእ ነጸግቲን ዓመጽን ፈሊጡ ከሎ እኳ፣ ቀዳማይ ስሚዒት የሱስ ርሕራሔ ነበረ — ሓዘን ንነፍሱ ዘይኮነ ግን ነቶም ካብ ፍቕሪ ባዕሉ ዝኸዱ።

Iziom hid'i zibelu nib'at ayneberun gin amik' nay hazen bikhiyat neberu n'hanti ketema b'milu'e libu zefekira. "N'selamki zitekm inte tifeltsi tiray" ilu i'wiyu. Abzi amik' neger biza'iba libi Amlak niri'i ina — neti zimets'e netsegtin ametsin felitu kelo ikwa, kedamay smi'it Yesus rihrahe nebere — hazen n'nefsu zeyikone gin n'itom kab fikri ba'ilu zikhedu.
Scene 3 of 25
[PICTURE: Jesus overturning tables in the temple courtyard, coins scattering through the air, doves flying free from their cages. Money changers scrambling back in fear. Jesus's face shows righteous intensity. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Jesus entered the temple and what He saw ignited a holy fire within Him. The outer court — the only place where Gentiles could come to pray — had been transformed into a noisy marketplace. Money changers charged outrageous fees to poor pilgrims, merchants sold sacrificial animals at inflated prices, and the religious leaders profited from it all. Jesus fashioned a whip and drove them out, overturning tables and scattering coins across the stone floor. "My house shall be called a house of prayer," He thundered, "but you have made it a den of robbers!" This wasn't uncontrolled rage — it was the righteous anger of a God who sees the powerful exploiting the vulnerable. Sometimes love looks like confrontation, like standing up against systems that crush the poor and barrier people from God's presence.
የሱስ ናብ ቤተ መቕደስ ኣትዩ ዝረኣዮ ነገር ቅዱስ ሓዊ ኣብ ውሽጡ ኣንዲዱ። እቲ ናይ ደገ ቀጽሪ — ኣህዛብ ክጽልዩ ዝኽእሉ ፍሉይ ቦታ — ናብ ጫውጫው ዕዳጋ ተቐይሩ ነበረ። ሸራፊ ገንዘብ ንድኻ ፓትርያሪክ ዘይርትዓዊ ክፍሊት ይሓቱ ነበሩ፣ ነጋዶ ድማ መስዋእቲ እንስሳ ብዝለዓለ ዋጋ ይሸጡ ነበሩ። የሱስ ሓንቲ ሶጢ ሰሪሑ ኩሎም ሰጒጉዎም፣ ጠረጴዛታት ገልቢጡ ናቕፋታት ኣብ እምኒ ባይታ ፋሕ ኣቢሉ። "ቤተይ ቤት ጸሎት ክብሃል እዩ" ኢሉ ነጒዱ፣ "ንስኻትኩም ግን ናይ ሸፋቱ በዓቲ ጌርኩምዎ!" እዚ ዘይተቖጻጸረ ቁጥዓ ኣይነበረን — ናይቲ ሓያላት ንድኹማት ክምዝምዙ ዝርኢ ኣምላኽ ጽድቂ ቁጥዓ ነበረ።
Yesus nab Bete Mekdes atiyu zirey'ayo neger kidus hawi ab wishtu andidu. Iti nay dege ketsri — Ahzab kitselyu zikhilu fluiy bota — nab chawchaw idaga tekeyiru nebere. Sherafi genzeb n'dikha patriarch zeyrit'awi kifliyit yihatu neberu, negado dima meswa'iti insa b'zile'ale waga yishetu neberu. Yesus hanti soti serihu kulom seguguwom, terepezatat gelibitu nakfatat ab imni bayta fah abilu. "Betey bet tselot kibhal iyu" ilu negwidu, "niskhatikum gin nay shefatu be'ati gerkumwo!" Izi zeytekotssatsere kut'a ayneberem — nayti hayalat n'dikhmat kimizimuzu ziri'i Amlak tsidki kut'a nebere.
Scene 4 of 25
[PICTURE: Jesus kneeling on the floor with a basin of water and towel, gently washing Peter's feet. The other disciples watch in stunned silence around a low table with bread and wine. Lamplight fills the upper room. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
On the night before His death, Jesus gathered His twelve closest friends in a borrowed upper room for the Passover meal. But before they ate, He did something that shocked them all — He removed His outer garment, wrapped a towel around His waist, poured water into a basin, and began to wash their feet. This was the work of the lowest servant, yet here was their Teacher and Lord kneeling before them. Peter protested, "You will never wash my feet!" But Jesus replied, "Unless I wash you, you have no part with me." In this single act, Jesus redefined power and leadership forever. The greatest among you, He taught, is not the one who is served, but the one who serves. He was showing them — and us — what love looks like when it puts on an apron and gets on its knees.
ኣብ ለይቲ ቅድሚ ሞቱ፣ የሱስ ነቶም ዓሰርተ ክልተ ቀረባ ፈተውቱ ኣብ ዝተለቃሕ ላዕለዋይ ክፍሊ ንመዝገብ ፋሲካ ኣኪቡዎም። ግን ቅድሚ ምብላዖም፣ ንኹሎም ዘደንጸወ ነገር ገይሩ — ናይ ደገ ክዳኑ ኣውጺኡ፣ ሕዙ ኣብ ሸረኩ ጠቕሊሉ፣ ማይ ኣብ ገበታ ኣፍሲሱ፣ ኣእጋሮም ክሓጽብ ጀሚሩ። እዚ ናይቲ ዝተሓተ ኣገልጋሊ ስራሕ ነበረ፣ ግን መምህሮምን ጐይታኦምን ቅድሚኦም ተምበርኪኹ ነበረ። ጴጥሮስ "ንስኻ ፈጺምካ ኣእጋረይ ኣይትሓጽቦምን!" ኢሉ ተቓዊሙ። የሱስ ግን "ኣነ እንተ ዘይሓጸብኩኻ ምሳይ ክፍሊ የብልካን" ኢሉ መሊሱ። ኣብዚ ሓደ ተግባር፣ የሱስ ንሓይልን መራሕነትን ንዘለኣለም ዳግም ገሊጹ።

Izi nayti zitehate agelgali sirah nebere, gin memhiromn goyita'omn kidmiom temberkiku nebere. Petros "Niskha fetsimka i'garey aytihatsbomn!" ilu tekawmu. [PICTURE: Jesus at the table breaking bread, holding it up with reverence. A cup of wine sits before Him glowing deep red. The disciples lean in, their faces illuminated by candlelight, expressions of wonder and solemnity. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Then Jesus took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and gave it to His disciples with words that would echo through two thousand years of history: "This is my body, which is broken for you. Do this in remembrance of me." Then He took the cup of wine: "This cup is the new covenant in my blood, which is poured out for you for the forgiveness of sins." In this moment, Jesus transformed an ancient Passover meal into something entirely new — a memorial of His coming sacrifice. Just as the original Passover lamb's blood had saved Israel from death in Egypt, His blood would save all humanity from the power of sin and death. Every time believers share bread and wine together, they remember: love was broken open so we could be made whole. This wasn't just a ritual — it was Jesus inviting us into His story forever.
ድሕሪኡ የሱስ ንእንጀራ ወሲዱ፣ ኣመስጊኑ፣ ቆሪሱ፣ ንደቀ መዛሙርቱ ሂቡዎም ምስ ቃላት ንኽልተ ሽሕ ዓመታት ታሪኽ ዝደሃሉ፥ "እዚ ንኣኻትኩም ዝተሰብረ ስጋይ እዩ። ንመዘከርታይ እዚ ግበሩ።" ድሕሪኡ ንጽዋእ ወይኒ ወሲዱ፥ "እዚ ጽዋእ ንሕድገት ሓጢኣት ንኣኻትኩም ዝፈስስ ብደመይ ዝኾነ ሓድሽ ኪዳን እዩ።" ኣብዚ ህሞት፣ የሱስ ንጥንታዊ ምግቢ ፋሲካ ናብ ሙሉእ ብሙሉእ ሓድሽ ነገር ቀይሩ — ናይ ዝመጽእ መስዋእቱ መዘከርታ። ከምቲ ናይ ፋሲካ ገንሸል ደም ንእስራኤል ካብ ሞት ኣብ ግብጺ ዘድሓኖም፣ ደሙ ንኹሉ ሰብ ካብ ሓይሊ ሓጢኣትን ሞትን ከድሕን ነበሮ። ፍቕሪ ተሰቢሩ ተኸፊቱ ንሕና ምሉኣት ምእንቲ ክንከውን።

[PICTURE: Jesus prostrate on the ground in the Garden of Gethsemane among ancient olive trees, His face pressed into the earth in agonized prayer. Moonlight filters through gnarled branches. After supper, Jesus led His disciples to the Garden of Gethsemane — a quiet olive grove on the Mount of Olives where He often went to pray. But this night was different. An overwhelming dread fell upon Him, and He told Peter, James, and John, "My soul is crushed with grief to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch with me." Then He went a little farther, fell face-down on the ground, and prayed with such intensity that His sweat fell like drops of blood. The full weight of what was coming pressed down upon Him — not just physical torture, but the spiritual agony of bearing every sin ever committed by every person who would ever live. The Son of God, who had known nothing but perfect communion with the Father, was about to experience total separation from that love. This was the cup He asked to be taken from Him.
ድሕሪ ድራር፣ የሱስ ንደቀ መዛሙርቱ ናብ ገነት ጌቴሴማኒ መሪሑዎም — ኣብ ደብረ ዘይቲ ዝርከብ ህዱእ ናይ ኣውሊዕ ስፍራ ብዙሕ ግዜ ክጽሊ ዝኸዶ። ግን እዚ ለይቲ ፍሉይ ነበረ። ዘይተጸበዮ ፍርሒ ወዲቕዎ፣ ንጴጥሮስ፣ ያእቆብ፣ ዮሃንስ ከኣ "ነፍሰይ ክሳብ ሞት ብሓዘን ተጨፍሊቓ ኣላ። ኣብዚ ጽንሑ ምሳይ ከኣ ንቕሑ" ኢሉዎም። ድሕሪኡ ቁሩብ ርሒቑ፣ ብገጹ ናብ መሬት ወዲቑ፣ ረሃጹ ከም ነጥቢ ደም ዝወድቕ ብዝኾነ ጽዕቶ ጸሊዩ። ምሉእ ክብደት ናይቲ ዝመጽእ — ናይ ኣካላዊ ስቓይ ጥራይ ዘይኮነ ግን ናይ ነፍሲ ስቓይ ናይ ኩሉ ሓጢኣት ምጽዋር — ኣብ ልዕሊኡ ጸቒጡ ነበረ።

At dawn, Jesus was dragged before Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor — because only Rome had the authority to execute. Pilate was a political animal who cared about power, not justice, yet even he could see that Jesus was innocent. "I find no fault in this man," he declared after questioning Him. Pilate's wife sent an urgent message: "Have nothing to do with this righteous man, for I have suffered greatly in a dream because of Him." But the religious leaders had stirred up the crowd, and the political pressure was immense. Pilate tried to find a way out — he sent Jesus to Herod, he offered to release Him — but he lacked the moral courage to do what he knew was right. Pilate represents everyone who recognizes truth but refuses to stand for it when the cost is too high. He could have freed Jesus with a word, but he feared the crowd more than he feared God.

Pilate had one last strategy: the Passover custom of releasing a prisoner. He offered the crowd a choice — Jesus, or Barabbas, a violent insurrectionist and murderer. In this terrible exchange, we see a picture of what the cross actually accomplishes: the guilty goes free while the innocent takes his place. Barabbas walked out of prison that day because Jesus took his cross. This is substitution — and it's what Jesus does for every one of us. Thomas was not there when Jesus first appeared, and when the others told him, he refused to believe: "Unless I see the nail marks in His hands, put my finger where the nails were, and put my hand into His side, I will not believe." A week later, Jesus appeared again — and went straight to Thomas. "Put your finger here; see my hands. Reach out your hand and put it into my side. Stop doubting and believe." Thomas fell to his knees: "My Lord and my God!" — the most profound declaration of faith in all the Gospels. Then Jesus said something for every person who would come after: "Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed." This is us. We are the blessed ones Jesus spoke of — those who believe without physically seeing. Doubt is not the enemy of faith; it's often the pathway to deeper faith. Jesus didn't reject Thomas for doubting; He met him in his doubt and gave him what he needed. He will do the same for us.

The Heart of the Story

Sacrificial love transforms everything.

The cross and resurrection together tell us the greatest truth in the universe: God loves you so much that He was willing to die for you — and His love is so powerful that even death could not contain it. Jesus's sacrifice was not just an event two thousand years ago; it is an ongoing invitation to receive forgiveness, find new life, and be transformed from the inside out.

The resurrection proves that no darkness is final, no defeat is permanent, and no grave can hold what God has decided to raise. Whatever you face — guilt, fear, doubt, grief, or hopelessness — the empty tomb declares that God specializes in bringing life from death.

Key Verse: "But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us." — Romans 5:8

Discussion Questions for Teens

1. The Cost of Love: Jesus had the power to come down from the cross at any moment but chose to stay. What does it mean that love is a choice, not a feeling? Have you ever had to choose to love someone when it cost you something? How does understanding Jesus's choice change how you view sacrifice?

2. The Meaning of Resurrection: If Jesus truly rose from the dead, how should that change the way we live today? What areas of your life feel "dead" — dreams, relationships, hope — that you need to trust God can bring back to life? What does "resurrection hope" look like practically for a teenager?

3. Responding to Doubt: Thomas doubted until he saw proof, yet Jesus didn't reject him. How do you handle your own doubts about God, faith, or the Bible? Is it okay to have questions? How can honest doubt actually lead to stronger faith rather than weaker faith?

4. The Cost of Following: Jesus told His followers to take up their cross and follow Him. What might "carrying your cross" look like for you as a teenager today? What do you find hardest to surrender to God? How is Peter's failure and restoration an encouragement when you feel you've let God down?

5. Living in Resurrection Hope: The disciples went from terrified and hiding to boldly changing the world. What made the difference? How does the reality of the resurrection give you courage to face peer pressure, anxiety, loss, or uncertainty about the future? What is one way you can live as a "person of the empty tomb" this week?

A Prayer of Gratitude and Hope

Lord Jesus, I stand in awe of what You did for me. You left the glory of heaven, walked among us, and when the time came, You didn't turn away from the cross — You embraced it, for me, for all of us.

Thank You for a love so deep that it chose nails over freedom, forgiveness over vengeance, sacrifice over safety. Thank You that on the darkest day in history, when hope seemed buried in a sealed tomb, You were not finished.

Help me understand the depth of the cross — not just as a story I've heard, but as a reality that changes everything about who I am and how I live. When I doubt, meet me like You met Thomas. When I fail, restore me like You restored Peter. When I feel alone, remind me of Your promise: "I am with you always."

Give me the courage to live as a person of the resurrection — to carry hope into dark places, to offer forgiveness when it's costly, and to trust that You are making all things new. May the power that raised You from the dead be alive and working in me today.

In Your risen and reigning name, Amen.
