Scene 1 of 25
[PICTURE: A bustling ancient city of Ur with ziggurats and temples in the background. Abram stands apart from the crowd, looking upward toward the sky with a searching expression while others bow before stone idols. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
In the ancient city of Ur of the Chaldeans — one of the most advanced civilizations of its time — a man named Abram lived among idol worshippers. The city boasted towering ziggurats, sophisticated trade routes, and a deeply entrenched polytheistic culture. Yet something stirred within Abram's spirit, an unshakable sense that the stone gods surrounding him were empty and powerless. He could not yet name what he was searching for, but his soul hungered for something — Someone — beyond the visible world. Sometimes the journey of faith begins not with answers, but with a holy dissatisfaction with the way things are.
ኣብታ ጥንታዊት ከተማ ዑር ናይ ከለዳውያን — ናይ ግዜኣ ካብ ዝማዕበሉ ስልጣነታት ሓደ — ኣብራም ዝስሙ ሰብኣይ ኣብ ማእከል ኣምለኽቲ ጣኦት ይነብር ነበረ። እታ ኸተማ ነዊሕ ዚግጉራታት፡ ዝማዕበለ ናይ ንግዲ መስመራት፡ ከምኡ'ውን ዝሰረጸ ናይ ብዙሕ ኣማልኽቲ ባህሊ ነበራ። ይኹን'ምበር ኣብ ውሽጢ መንፈስ ኣብራም ሓደ ነገር ተዋስኦ፡ እቶም ዝከቡቦ ናይ እምኒ ኣማልኽቲ ባዶን ስልጣን ዘይብሎምን ምዃኖም ዘይሕዝ ስምዒት። ዝደልዮ ነገር ገና ክሰምዮ ኣይክኣለን፡ ግን ነፍሱ ካብ ርኡይ ዓለም ንላዕሊ ዝኾነ ነገር — ንሓደ — ጠምየ ነበረት። ሓደ ሓደ ግዜ ጉዕዞ እምነት ብመልሲ ዘይኮነ ብቕዱስ ዘይዕግበት ናይ ከም ዘሎ ኩነታት ይጅምር።
(Ab'ta tintawit ketema Ur nay Keledawyan — nay gize'a kab zimaabellu silttantat hade — Abram zismu seb'ay ab ma'ekel amlekti ta'ot yinebir nebere.)
Scene 2 of 25
[PICTURE: A divine light breaking through dark clouds and illuminating Abram who stands alone in an open field at night. The light forms a path leading away from the city of Ur visible in the distance. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Then God spoke. Out of the silence of centuries, the Creator of the universe addressed one man with words that would reshape human history: "Leave your country, your people, and your father's household, and go to the land I will show you." No map. No coordinates. No timeline. Just a voice, a command, and an invitation to trust. God was asking Abram to abandon every source of security he had ever known — his homeland, his extended family, his cultural identity, his inheritance — and walk into complete uncertainty with nothing but a divine promise as his guide.
ድሕሪኡ ኣምላኽ ተዛረበ። ካብ ጸጥታ ናይ ዘመናት፡ ፈጣሪ ዓለም ንሓደ ሰብ ታሪኽ ደቂ ሰብ ዝቕይር ቃላት ተዛረቦ፡ "ካብ ሃገርካ፡ ካብ ህዝብኻ፡ ካብ ቤት ኣቦኻ ውጻእ፡ ናብ ዘርእየካ ምድሪ ኺድ።" ካርታ የለን። ኣንፈት የለን። ናይ ግዜ ሰሌዳ የለን። ድምጺ ጥራይ፡ ትእዛዝ፡ ከምኡ'ውን ንምእማን ዕድመ። ኣምላኽ ንኣብራም ዝፈልጦ ኩሉ ምንጪ ውሕስነት ክሓድግ ይሓቶ ነበረ — ሃገሩ፡ ስድራቤቱ፡ ባህላዊ መንነቱ፡ ውርሻኡ — ከምኡ'ውን ናይ ኣምላኽ ተስፋ ጥራይ ከም መራሒኡ ሒዙ ናብ ምሉእ ዘይርግጸት ክኸይድ።
(Dihr'iu Amlakh tezarebe. Kab tsettita nay zemanat, Fetari alem nihade seb tarikh deqi seb ziqeyir qalat tezarebo.)
Scene 3 of 25
[PICTURE: God's promises visualized as golden light surrounding Abram - images of a great nation (many people), stars in the sky, and families of all nations being blessed radiating outward from Abram. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
With the command came extraordinary promises — promises so vast they seemed impossible for one elderly man to comprehend: "I will make you into a great nation. I will bless you and make your name great. You will be a blessing. I will bless those who bless you and curse those who curse you. All peoples on earth will be blessed through you." These were not modest promises. God was declaring that through this one act of obedience, through this single man's willingness to step into the unknown, every nation and every generation — including ours — would be forever changed. The weight of that covenant was staggering.
ምስ ትእዛዝ ፍሉያት ተስፋታት መጻ — ንሓደ ዝኣረገ ሰብ ክርድኦ ዘይከኣል ዝመስል ሰፊሕ ተስፋታት፡ "ዓቢ ሃገር ክገብረካ እየ። ክባርኸካ ስምካ'ውን ዓቢ ክገብሮ እየ። በረኸት ክትከውን ኢኻ። ንዝባርኹኻ ክባርኾም ንዝመርገሙኻ ክረግሞም እየ። ኩሎም ህዝብታት ምድሪ ብኣኻ ክባረኹ እዮም።" እዚኣተን ትሑት ተስፋታት ኣይነበራን። ኣምላኽ ብዛዕባ እዚ ሓደ ናይ ምእዛዝ ተግባር፡ ብዛዕባ እዚ ናይ ሓደ ሰብ ድሌት ናብ ዘይተፈልጠ ክኣቱ፡ ኩሉ ሃገርን ኩሉ ወለዶን — ናትና ሓዊሱ — ንሓዋሩ ክቕየር ምዃኑ ይእውጅ ነበረ። ክብደት ናይቲ ቃል ኪዳን ዘደንጹ ነበረ።
(Mis ti'izaz filuyat tesfatat metsa — nihade zi'arege seb kird'o zeyk'al zimesil sefih tesfatat.)
Scene 4 of 25
[PICTURE: Abram at age 75, with his wife Sarai and nephew Lot, loading camels and donkeys with their possessions. Servants helping prepare for departure. The city of Haran visible behind them as they face an open road ahead. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran — an age when most people seek comfort and stability, not radical upheaval. He took his wife Sarai, his nephew Lot, all their possessions, and the servants they had acquired. Consider what this meant: at an age when retirement beckons, Abram chose adventure. When certainty called, he chose mystery. When comfort whispered, he chose obedience. The text says simply, "So Abram went, as the Lord had told him." No recorded argument. No negotiation. No demand for more details. Just obedience. This is what mature faith looks like — not blind, but willing.
ኣብራም ካብ ሓራን ክወጽእ ሰብዓ ሓሙሽተ ዓመቱ ነበረ — ብዙሓት ሰባት ምቾትን ርግኣትን ዝደልዩሉ ዕድመ፡ ሰረት ዝብትኽ ለውጢ ዘይኮነ። ሰበይቱ ሳራይ፡ ወዲ ሓው ሎጥ፡ ኩሉ ንብረቶም፡ ከምኡ'ውን ዝረኸቦም ኣገልገልቲ ወሰደ። እዚ እንታይ ማለት ከም ዝነበረ ሕሰብ፡ ጡረታ ዝጽውዓሉ ዕድመ ኣብራም ዘይፍለጥ ጉዕዞ መረጸ። ርግጸት ምስ ጸወዐ ምስጢር መረጸ። ምቾት ምስ ሓሸሸ ምእዛዝ መረጸ። ጽሑፍ ብቐሊሉ "ከምቲ ኣምላኽ ዝበሎ ኣብራም ከደ" ይብል። ዝተመዝገበ ክትዕ የለን። ድርድር የለን። ተወሳኺ ዝርዝር ጠለብ የለን። ምእዛዝ ጥራይ። ብሱል እምነት ከምዚ ይመስል — ዕውር ዘይኮነ ግን ፍቓደኛ።
(Abram kab Haran kiwets'e seb'a hamusht amettu nebere — bizuhat sebat mich'otin rig'atin zideliyullu edme.)
Scene 5 of 25
[PICTURE: Abram's caravan traveling through vast, empty desert landscape. A long line of camels, donkeys, and people stretching across golden sand dunes under a wide open sky. The journey feels both lonely and majestic. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
The journey from Haran to Canaan was roughly four hundred miles — weeks of travel through unfamiliar terrain with no GPS, no established roads, and no guarantee of safety. Every step took Abram further from everything familiar and deeper into dependence on God alone. This is the nature of faith: it is not a single decision made once, but a daily choice to keep walking when the destination remains invisible. Each morning Abram woke up and chose to continue. Each night he slept under foreign stars and trusted that God's word was more reliable than his own understanding.
ካብ ሓራን ናብ ከነኣን ዝኸውን ጉዕዞ ኣስታት ኣርባዕ ሚእቲ ማይልስ ነበረ — ሰሙናት ናይ ጉዕዞ ብዘይተፈልጠ ቦታ ብዘይ ጂፒኤስ፡ ብዘይ ዝተመስረተ መንገዲ፡ ከምኡ'ውን ብዘይ ናይ ድሕነት ውሕስነት። ነፍሲ ወከፍ ስጉምቲ ንኣብራም ካብ ዝፈልጦ ኩሉ ኣርሒቑ ኣብ ኣምላኽ ጥራይ ናብ ምምርኮስ ኣእተዎ። ባህሪ እምነት ከምዚ እዩ፡ ሓንሳእ ዝውሰድ ውሳኔ ዘይኮነ መዓልታዊ ናይ ምኻድ ምርጫ መብጻሕያ ዘይርአ ከሎ። ነፍሲ ወከፍ ንግሆ ኣብራም ተንሲኡ ክቕጽል መረጸ። ነፍሲ ወከፍ ለይቲ ኣብ ትሕቲ ወጻኢ ከዋኽብቲ ደቂሱ ቃል ኣምላኽ ካብ ናቱ ርድኢት ዝያዳ ዝእመን ምዃኑ ኣመነ።
(Kab Haran nab Kene'an zikewn gu'zo astat arba'e mi'iti mayls nebere — semunt nay gu'zo bizeytefeltte bota.)
Scene 6 of 25
[PICTURE: Abram arriving in Canaan, standing on a hilltop overlooking a green and fertile land. He builds a stone altar under a great oak tree at Shechem, with his hands raised in worship. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
When Abram arrived in Canaan, God appeared to him at the great tree of Moreh at Shechem and said, "To your offspring I will give this land." There was one significant problem: Abram had no offspring. He was seventy-five, Sarai was sixty-five, and they were childless. Yet Abram built an altar there — an act of worship and a stake in the ground declaring he believed God's promise even when his circumstances contradicted it completely. Faith often requires us to worship before we see the fulfillment, to give thanks for what exists only as a promise.
ኣብራም ኣብ ከነኣን ምስ በጽሐ፡ ኣምላኽ ኣብ ዓባይ ኦም ሞሬ ኣብ ሸኬም ተራእዮ "ነዛ ምድሪ ንዘርእኻ ክህባ እየ" በሎ። ሓደ ዓቢ ጸገም ነበረ፡ ኣብራም ዘርኢ ኣይነበሮን። ሰብዓ ሓሙሽተ ዓመቱ ነበረ፡ ሳራይ ስሳ ሓሙሽተ ዓመታ፡ ከምኡ'ውን ቆልዑ ኣይነበሮምን። ይኹን'ምበር ኣብራም ኣብኡ መሰውኢ ሰርሐ — ናይ ኣምልኾ ተግባርን ኩነታቱ ብምሉእ ዝጻረሮ እንተ ኾነ'ውን ንተስፋ ኣምላኽ ይኣምን ከም ዘሎ ዝእውጅ ምልክትን። እምነት ብዙሕ ግዜ ቅድሚ ምፍጻም ምርኣይ ክነምልኽ፡ ከም ተስፋ ጥራይ ንዘሎ ከነመስግን ይሓተና።
(Abram ab Kene'an mis bets'he, Amlakh ab abay om More ab Shekem tera'eyo "neza midri nizeri'ika kihba iye" belo.)

[PICTURE: Abram standing outside his tent at night, God's presence represented by warm light above him. Abram gazes at an impossibly vast night sky filled with countless brilliant stars. Years passed — perhaps ten or more — and still no child. One night, God came to Abram in a vision: "Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your very great reward." But Abram, for the first time, voiced his frustration honestly: "Sovereign Lord, what can you give me since I remain childless?" God did not rebuke this honesty. Instead, He took Abram outside and said, "Look up at the sky and count the stars — if indeed you can count them. So shall your offspring be." And Abram believed the Lord, and it was credited to him as righteousness. This is the pivotal moment — faith was not perfection but trust despite unanswered questions.

The Moral of the Story / ናይ ታሪኽ ትምህርቲ

Faith is not the absence of doubt but obedience despite it. Abraham doubted, laughed, questioned, and even tried to help God with human solutions — yet he is called the father of faith because when it mattered most, he obeyed. God's promises have no expiration date; twenty-five years of waiting did not cancel the covenant. Waiting is not wasted time — it is preparation time, shaping us into people capable of carrying the blessing. Trying to help God with human shortcuts creates complications that ripple through generations (Ishmael). True faith is tested, not theoretical — untested faith is merely an opinion. And God never asks us to give up something without giving back something greater. The ram replaced Isaac; the blessing exceeded the sacrifice. Trust the character of God when you cannot trace the plan of God.

Discussion Questions for Teens / ንመንእሰያት ናይ ምይይጥ ሕቶታት

  1. Has God ever asked you to leave something comfortable or familiar behind in order to grow? What did that feel like, and how did you respond? How might Abraham's example of immediate obedience challenge the way you handle God's direction in your life?
  2. Abraham waited twenty-five years for Isaac. What is the longest you have ever waited for something you believed God promised? How do you maintain faith during extended seasons of waiting when nothing seems to be happening? What does Abraham's story teach about God's timing versus ours?
  3. When Sarai suggested using Hagar, Abraham agreed to a human shortcut. Can you think of times when you've tried to "help God" by forcing outcomes rather than waiting for His timing? What were the consequences? How can we distinguish between wise action and impatient human engineering?
  4. Abraham laughed at God's promise, and God wasn't offended — He even named Isaac "laughter." What does this tell us about how God handles our honest doubts? Is there a difference between honest questioning and rebellious unbelief? How can we bring our doubts to God without losing faith?
  5. The ultimate test required Abraham to surrender Isaac — the very thing God had promised. What is the "Isaac" in your life — the thing you love so much it might rival your trust in God? Why do you think God sometimes asks us to hold our greatest blessings with open hands rather than clenched fists?
