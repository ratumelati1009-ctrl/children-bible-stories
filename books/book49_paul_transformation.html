Scene 1
[PICTURE: A young, intense man named Saul studying Torah scrolls in a grand Jerusalem academy. He wears the robes of a Pharisee, his eyes burning with intellectual fire. Saul of Tarsus was no ordinary man. Born a Roman citizen in a Greek city, educated under Gamaliel — the most respected rabbi of his generation — Saul was a rising star in Judaism. He was a Pharisee's Pharisee: brilliant in debate, passionate about the Law, and utterly convinced that his zeal for God made him righteous. He had memorized vast portions of Scripture, spoke multiple languages, and burned with an intensity that set him apart from his peers. Saul was the kind of person everyone expected would change the world — they just didn't know how.

Scene 2
[PICTURE: A crowd of angry men dragging a young man named Stephen outside the city walls. Stephen's face is radiant and peaceful, looking upward toward heaven. The first time we meet Saul in Scripture, he is standing at a murder. Stephen — a young believer filled with the Holy Spirit — had preached boldly about Jesus being the Messiah. The religious leaders couldn't bear it. They dragged Stephen outside the city and began hurling stones at him. Saul didn't throw the stones himself, but he held the coats of those who did, giving his full approval. As Stephen died, he looked up and said, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them." Saul heard those words. A man being murdered was forgiving his killers. Something about that must have lodged deep in Saul's consciousness, even if he refused to acknowledge it.
ኣብ ቅዱስ ጽሑፍ ንመጀመርታ ግዜ ምስ ንራኽቦ ሳውል ኣብ ቅትለት ደው ኢሉ'ዩ ዝነበረ። እስጢፋኖስ — ብመንፈስ ቅዱስ ዝመልአ ሓደ መንእሰይ ኣማኒ — የሱስ መሲሕ ምዃኑ ብድፍረት ሰቢኹ ነበረ። እቶም ናይ ሃይማኖት መራሕቲ ክጻወርዎ ኣይከኣሉን። ንእስጢፋኖስ ካብ ከተማ ጎቲቶም ኣእማን ክድርብይዎ ጀመሩ። ሳውል ባዕሉ ኣእማን ኣይደርበየን ግን ናይቶም ዝደርበዩ ክዳውንቲ ሒዙ ምሉእ ስምምዕነቱ ሂቡ። እስጢፋኖስ ክመውት ከሎ ንላዕሊ ጠሚቱ "ጐይታ እዛ ሓጢኣት ኣይትሓዘሎም" በለ። ሳውል ነዘን ቃላት ሰምዐን። ዝቕተል ዝነበረ ሰብ ንቐተልቱ ይቕረ ይብል ነበረ። ዋላ ከላለዮ እንተ ኣበየ ገለ ነገር ብዛዕብኡ ኣብ ኣእምሮ ሳውል ዓሚቝ ክኣቱ ኣለዎ።

Scene 3
[PICTURE: Saul leading armed temple guards through Jerusalem's streets at night, breaking into homes. Terrified Christian families are being dragged out — men, women, and children crying. After Stephen's death, Saul didn't pause to reflect — he escalated. He began systematically destroying the church in Jerusalem. Going house to house, he dragged out men and women and threw them into prison. Families were torn apart. Children watched their parents hauled away. Saul did this not out of cruelty for its own sake, but out of sincere — yet terribly misguided — religious conviction. He genuinely believed he was serving God by eliminating what he saw as a dangerous heresy. This is perhaps the most frightening lesson of Saul's story: passion without love, zeal without wisdom, can make a person capable of terrible evil while believing they are doing good.
ድሕሪ ሞት እስጢፋኖስ ሳውል ደው ኢሉ ኣይሓሰበን — ኣበርተዐ። ኣብ ኢየሩሳሌም ንቤተ ክርስትያን ብስርዓት ከጥፍእ ጀመረ። ገዛ ንገዛ እናኸደ ሰብኡትን ኣንስትን ጎቲቱ ናብ ቤት ማእሰርቲ ደርበዮም። ስድራቤታት ተፈላልዩ። ቆልዑ ወለዶም ክግፍዑ ረአዩ። ሳውል ነዚ ንጭካኔ ባዕሉ ዘይኮነ ብቅኑዕ — ግን ብኣዝዩ ዝተጋገየ — ናይ ሃይማኖት እምነት'ዩ ዝገበሮ። ብሓቂ ንኣምላኽ ከም ዘገልግል ይኣምን ነበረ ሓደገኛ ክሕደት ዝረኣዮ ብምጥፋእ። እዚ ምናልባት ናይ ዛንታ ሳውል ዝበለጸ ዘፍርህ ትምህርቲ'ዩ፡ ብዘይ ፍቕሪ ህርፋን ብዘይ ጥበብ ቅንኢ ሓደ ሰብ ጽቡቕ ይገብር ኣለኹ ብዝብል እምነት ዘፍርህ ክፍኣት ክገብር ክእለት ክህቦ ይኽእል።

Scene 4
[PICTURE: Saul standing before the High Priest in the ornate temple chambers, receiving official letters with seals. His expression is eager and determined. Guards stand ready behind him with chains and weapons. A map showing the road to Damascus hangs on the wall. East African/Eritrean features. Warm watercolor children's Bible illustration.]
Not content with persecuting Christians in Jerusalem alone, Saul's ambition grew. He went to the High Priest and requested official letters authorizing him to arrest any followers of "the Way" — as early Christians were called — in Damascus, over 200 kilometers away. His plan was to bind them and bring them back to Jerusalem for trial and punishment. Saul was breathing murderous threats against the Lord's disciples. His hatred had become his identity, his mission, his reason for living. He was a man consumed by what he thought was righteous fury — completely unaware that he was fighting against the very God he claimed to serve.
ኣብ ኢየሩሳሌም ጥራይ ክርስትያናት ምስጓግ ኣየዕገቦን ሳውል ሸውሃቱ ዓበየ። ናብ ሊቀ ካህናት ከይዱ ኣብ ደማስቆ — ልዕሊ 200 ኪሎ ሜትሮ ርሒቑ — ንዝኾነ ናይ "መንገዲ" — ናይ ቀዳሞት ክርስትያናት ስም — ተኸታሊ ንኽእስር ዘፍቅዱ ወግዓዊ ደብዳቤታት ሓተተ። ኣሲሩ ንፍርድን ቅጽዓትን ናብ ኢየሩሳሌም ክመልሶም'ዩ ዝነበረ ውጥኑ። ሳውል ኣብ ልዕሊ ደቂ መዛሙርቲ ጐይታ ቀታሊ ምፍርራህ ይተንፍስ ነበረ። ጽልኡ መንነቱ ተልእኾኡ ናይ ምንባር ምኽንያቱ ኮይኑ ነበረ። ጻድቕ ቁጥዐ ዝመስሎ ዝበልዖ ሰብ ነበረ — ዘገልግሎ ኣለኹ ዝብሎ ዝነበረ ኣምላኽ ባዕሉ ይቃለስ ከም ዝነበረ ፍጹም ዘይፈልጥ።

Scene 5

It happened near Damascus, around noon, when the sun was already at its brightest. Suddenly, a light from heaven — brighter than the midday sun — blazed around Saul. It was not natural light; it was the raw, unfiltered glory of the risen Christ breaking into the physical world. Saul collapsed to the ground, blinded and overwhelmed. His traveling companions heard a sound but saw no one. In a single moment, everything Saul thought he knew, everything he had built his life upon, was shattered. The hunter had become the hunted. The persecutor had been found by the One he was persecuting.
ኣብ ቀረባ ደማስቆ ኣብ ቀትሪ ጸሓይ ድሮ ኣብ ዝበርትዐሉ ግዜ ተፈጸመ። ብድንገት ብርሃን ካብ ሰማይ — ካብ ቀትራዊ ጸሓይ ዝበልጽ — ኣብ ዙርያ ሳውል ነደደ። ተፈጥሮኣዊ ብርሃን ኣይነበረን፣ ናብ ቁሳዊ ዓለም ዝኣተወ ናይ ዝተንስአ ክርስቶስ ጥሪኡ ዘይተጸራረየ ክብሪ'ዩ ዝነበረ። ሳውል ናብ ምድሪ ወዲቑ ዓይኑ ጸልሚቱ ተሰኒፉ። ናይ ጕዕዞ ብጾቱ ድምጺ ሰምዑ ግን ማንም ኣይረአዩን። ኣብ ሓንቲ ህሞት ሳውል ዝፈልጦ ዝነበረ ኩሉ ነገር ሂወቱ ዝሃነጸሉ ኩሉ ነገር ተደምሲሱ። እቲ ሃዳኒ ተሃዲኑ'ዩ። እቲ ሰጓጊ ብእቲ ዝሰጎ ዝነበረ ተረኺቡ።

Then came the voice — personal, piercing, impossible to ignore: "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute ME?" Not "why do you persecute my followers" or "why do you persecute my church" — but ME. In that single word, Jesus revealed something revolutionary: He so completely identifies with His people that when they suffer, He suffers. Every Christian Saul had arrested, every believer he had beaten, every family he had torn apart — he had been doing it to Jesus Himself. Saul managed to whisper, "Who are you, Lord?" And the answer came: "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting." The One Saul thought was dead was alive. The One he was fighting against was God Himself.

Paul didn't just preach to people who already believed in God — he engaged the greatest thinkers of his age. In Athens, the intellectual capital of the ancient world, Paul stood before the Areopagus and addressed Epicurean and Stoic philosophers. He didn't dismiss their culture; he found a connection point — an altar inscribed "TO AN UNKNOWN GOD" — and used it to proclaim the true God. Paul's brilliance was his ability to communicate eternal truth in language each audience could understand. Paul's central message was revolutionary and scandalous: salvation comes through grace alone, received by faith — not through keeping the Law, not through human effort, not through religious performance. "For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith — and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God — not by works, so that no one can boast." This message was radical because Paul himself had tried the path of works harder than anyone. He had been the ultimate religious performer — and he knew from experience that it led only to pride, cruelty, and emptiness. Grace humbles us because it says: you cannot earn God's love. You can only receive it.

Even in his final imprisonment in Rome, Paul remained productive and joyful. His "prison epistles" — Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, and Philemon — contain some of the most beautiful and hopeful passages in all of Scripture. From a Roman cell, chained to guards, Paul wrote: "Rejoice in the Lord always. I will say it again: Rejoice!" He told the Philippians that his imprisonment had actually advanced the gospel because the entire palace guard had heard about Christ. Paul had learned the secret of contentment — not in circumstances changing, but in Christ being sufficient regardless of circumstances. His joy was not dependent on freedom; it was rooted in something no prison could contain.

Paul never forgot what he had been. He called himself "the worst of sinners" and "one abnormally born" — referring to the unusual, violent way he came to faith. The Moral of the Story

No one is too far gone for God's grace. God can transform our greatest weaknesses into our greatest strengths. The same passion that drives us wrong can be redirected for God's purposes. Past failures don't disqualify us from future calling — surrendered to God, they become our most powerful testimony. Discussion Questions / ሕቶታት ምይይጥ

1. Saul genuinely believed he was serving God while persecuting Christians. How can someone be sincere yet sincerely wrong? What safeguards can you put in place to make sure your passion and zeal are directed by love rather than by pride or fear?

1. ሳውል ክርስትያናት ክሰጉግ ከሎ ብሓቂ ንኣምላኽ ከም ዘገልግል ይኣምን ነበረ። ሓደ ሰብ ቅኑዕ ግን ብቕንዕና ዝተጋገየ ብኸመይ ክኸውን ይኽእል? ህርፋንካን ቅንኢኻን ብፍቕሪ'ምበር ብትዕቢት ወይ ብፍርሒ ከም ዝምራሕ ንምርግጋጽ እንታይ ሓለዋ ክትገብር ትኽእል?

2. Barnabas risked his reputation to vouch for Paul when no one else would trust him. Has anyone ever believed in you when others doubted you? How did that affect your life? Is there someone in your life right now who needs a "Barnabas" — and could you be that person for them?

2. በርናባስ ካልእ ማንም ዘይኣምኖ ከሎ ንጳውሎስ ንኽምስክር ክብሩ ኣብ ሓደጋ ኣውዓለ። ካልኦት ምስ ተጠራጠሩኻ ኣባኻ ዝኣመነ ሰብ ነይሩ'ዶ? ሂወትካ ብኸመይ ጸልዮ? ኣብ ሂወትካ ሕጂ "በርናባስ" ዘድልዮ ሰብ ኣሎ'ዶ — ንዕኡ እቲ ሰብ ክትከውን ትኽእል'ዶ?

3. Paul said "I am not ashamed of the gospel." What does it look like to be unashamed of your faith in today's world — at school, online, or among friends who don't believe? Is there a difference between being bold and being obnoxious about your faith?

3. ጳውሎስ "ብወንጌል ኣይሓፍርን'የ" በለ። ኣብ ናይ ሎሚ ዓለም — ኣብ ትምህርቲ ቤት ኣብ ኢንተርነት ወይ ኣብ መንጎ ዘይኣምኑ ኣዕሩኽ — ብእምነትካ ዘይምሕፋር ከመይ ይመስል? ብዛዕባ እምነትካ ደፋር ምዃንን ዘሕፍር ምዃንን ፍልልይ ኣሎ'ዶ?

4. Paul taught that grace — not works — saves us. Why is this message so hard for people to accept? Do you ever feel like you need to "earn" God's love or prove yourself worthy? How does understanding grace change the way you view yourself and others?

4. ጳውሎስ ጸጋ — ግብሪ ዘይኮነ — ከም ዘድሕነና ምሃረ። እዚ መልእኽቲ ሰባት ክቕበልዎ ንምንታይ ከቢድ'ዩ? ናይ ኣምላኽ ፍቕሪ "ክትረኽቦ" ወይ በቓዕ ምዃንካ ክተረጋግጽ ከም ዘለካ ዝስምዓካ ግዜ ኣሎ'ዶ? ጸጋ ምርዳእ ንገዛእ ርእስኻን ንኻልኦትን ትርእየሉ መገዲ ብኸመይ ይቕይር?

5. Paul's story shows that God doesn't waste our past — even our failures and sins can be repurposed for His glory. What experiences in your life — including mistakes or struggles — might God use for good in the future? How does this perspective change the way you view your own story?

5. ዛንታ ጳውሎስ ኣምላኽ ንሕሉፍና ከም ዘየባኽኖ የርኢ — ዋላ ውድቀታትናን ሓጢኣትናን ንኽብሩ ዳግም ዕላማ ክህቦ ይኽእል። ኣብ ሂወትካ — ጌጋታት ወይ ቃልሲ ሓዊስካ — ኣምላኽ ኣብ መጻኢ ንጽቡቕ ክጥቀመሉ ዝኽእል እንታይ ተሞክሮ ኣሎ? እዚ ኣረኣእያ ንናይ ገዛእ ርእስኻ ዛንታ ትርእየሉ መገዲ ብኸመይ ይቕይር?
