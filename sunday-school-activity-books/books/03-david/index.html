Lesson Aim & Teacher Preparation

Lesson aim: Children will learn that God gives us courage and strength to face big problems.

Learning objectives

  • Retell the story of David and Goliath.
  • Explain why David was not afraid.
  • Recite the memory verse (1 Samuel 17:45).
  • Name a 'giant' problem God can help them face.

You will need

  • Bibles or printed story pages
  • Crayons, scissors, glue
  • Paper and soft materials (cotton) for the craft
  • Optional: a measuring tape
Teacher tip: Have children measure themselves and compare to Goliath's height for a fun start.

Opening Prayer & Gathering

Welcome circle

Gather in a circle. Ask children what makes them feel small or scared, and reassure them God is bigger.

Dear God, you are stronger than anything we fear. Help us be brave like David and trust in you. Amen.

Icebreaker question

What is something that feels really big or scary to you?

The Bible Story — Part 1

The Shepherd Boy

David was the youngest of eight brothers. He looked after his father's sheep and played the harp.

When lions or bears attacked the flock, David protected them. God gave him courage and strength.

The Bible Story — Part 2

The Giant's Challenge

The Philistine army had a giant named Goliath, over nine feet tall. Every day he dared Israel to fight him.

All the soldiers, even the king, were terrified. No one was brave enough to face the giant.

The Bible Story — Part 3

David Is Not Afraid

David said, 'Who is this giant to defy the army of the living God?' He offered to fight Goliath.

'The Lord who saved me from the lion and the bear will save me,' David said. He took his sling and five smooth stones.

The Bible Story — Part 4

God Gives the Victory

Goliath laughed, but David said, 'You come with a sword, but I come in the name of the Lord!'

David swung his sling and the stone struck the giant. Goliath fell! God gave the victory to the boy who trusted him.

Talk About It

Discussion questions for the group

Sit in a circle. Let everyone share. There are no wrong answers when we talk about God's Word!

1. Why was David not afraid of the giant?

2. How had God helped David before?

3. What 'giant' problems feel too big for you?

4. How can God help us face hard things?

5. Why is it better to trust God than to trust our own strength?

Living It Out

Facing giants with God like David

Read each situation. Circle the choice that pleases God. Talk about it together.

1. A big problem feels too hard.

a) Ask God for help and try.   b) Give up.

2. Others laugh because you are small or young.

a) Feel worthless.   b) Remember God made you special.

3. You must stand up for what is right, alone.

a) Be brave with God's help.   b) Run away.

Act It Out!

Retell the story with a simple drama

Teacher tip: Give children simple roles and props. Acting the story helps them remember it.

Roles & steps

  1. Choose David, Goliath (make him 'tall' on tiptoe or a chair), King Saul and soldiers.
  2. The soldiers cower while Goliath 'shouts'.
  3. David steps forward and 'swings' his sling.
  4. Goliath falls and everyone celebrates God's victory!

Game: Beanbag Sling Toss

A fun game for the whole group

You need: soft beanbags or rolled socks, a target or bucket

How to play

  1. Set a bucket or target a few steps away.
  2. Each child gets five soft 'stones' to toss.
  3. Count how many land in the target.
  4. Cheer that David trusted God, not just his aim!
Teacher tip: Use only soft items and keep the throwing line clear for safety.

Craft: Paper Sling & Courage Stones

Make five courage stones!

You need: paper, crayons, cotton balls or crumpled paper, scissors
  1. Draw and cut out a sling shape.
  2. Make five soft 'stones' from cotton or paper.
  3. Write a courage word on each stone (Brave, Trust, Faith...).
  4. Remember: with God, you can face any giant!
