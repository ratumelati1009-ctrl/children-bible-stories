Lesson Aim & Teacher Preparation

Lesson aim: Children will learn that God gives us spiritual 'armor' to stand strong and do what is right.

Learning objectives

  • Name the pieces of the armor of God.
  • Explain what each piece helps us do.
  • Recite the memory verse (Ephesians 6:11).
  • Choose one piece of armor to 'wear' this week.

You will need

  • Bibles or printed lesson pages
  • Crayons, card, scissors, glue
  • Optional: cardboard for a shield and sword
Teacher tip: Teach each armor piece with a simple action so children remember them.

Opening Prayer & Gathering

Welcome circle

Gather in a circle. Ask children what protects a knight or soldier, then explain God protects us too.

Dear God, thank you for giving us your armor to stand strong. Help us to be brave and do right. Amen.

Icebreaker question

If you were a knight, which piece of armor would you want most, and why?

The Bible Story — Part 1

Standing Strong

The apostle Paul wrote a letter to teach Christians how to stand strong and do what is right.

He said God gives us special 'armor' — not made of metal, but to help our hearts and minds be strong in faith.

The Bible Story — Part 2

The Belt and the Breastplate

First is the belt of truth — always telling the truth and living honestly.

Next is the breastplate of righteousness — doing what is right, which guards our hearts like a breastplate guards the chest.

The Bible Story — Part 3

Shoes, Shield and Helmet

We wear shoes of peace, ready to share the good news of Jesus wherever we go.

The shield of faith protects us when we are tempted, and the helmet of salvation guards our minds, reminding us we belong to God.

The Bible Story — Part 4

The Sword of the Spirit

Last is the sword of the Spirit, which is God's Word, the Bible. We learn it and use it to know what is true.

When we 'put on' all of God's armor and pray, we can stand strong and do what is right, with God's help.

Memory Verse

“Put on the full armor of God so you can stand strong.”Ephesians 6:11 (ICB)
Read the verse together three times. Then trace it and say it from memory!

Put on the full armor of God so you can stand strong.

Teacher tip: Do a 'suit up' motion (belt, chest, feet, arm, head) as you review each armor piece.

Talk About It

Discussion questions for the group

Sit in a circle. Let everyone share. There are no wrong answers when we talk about God's Word!

1. Why do soldiers wear armor?

2. What does the belt of truth help us do?

3. How does the shield of faith help us?

4. Why is God's Word called a sword?

5. Which piece of armor do you most want to 'wear' this week?

Living It Out

Standing strong in God's armor

Read each situation. Circle the choice that pleases God. Talk about it together.

1. A friend wants you to lie for them.

a) Wear the belt of truth and be honest.   b) Lie to help.

2. You feel tempted to do something wrong.

a) Give in.   b) Raise the shield of faith and say no.

3. You are unsure what is right.

a) Read God's Word for help.   b) Just guess.

Act It Out!

Retell the story with a simple drama

Teacher tip: Give children simple roles and props. Acting the story helps them remember it.

Roles & steps

  1. Give children cardboard armor pieces: belt, breastplate, shoes, shield, helmet, sword.
  2. As the narrator names each piece, a child 'puts it on' with an action.
  3. The group stands together, 'strong' and ready.
  4. End by 'praying' together, the most important part of standing strong.

Game: Armor Relay

A fun game for the whole group

You need: paper armor pieces, two boxes

How to play

  1. Two teams race to collect armor pieces from a box.
  2. Each runner 'puts on' one piece and returns.
  3. The first team fully 'suited up' wins.
  4. Shout the memory verse together to finish.
Teacher tip: Name each piece as it's 'worn' so children link the game to the lesson.
