Lesson Aim & Teacher Preparation

Lesson aim: Children will learn to obey God and trust that he always keeps his promises.

Learning objectives

  • Retell the story of Noah's ark.
  • Explain why Noah obeyed God.
  • Recite the memory verse (Genesis 9:13).
  • Name one way to obey God this week.

You will need

  • Bibles or printed story pages
  • Crayons, scissors, glue
  • Cotton wool and paper for the rainbow craft
  • Optional: toy animals
Teacher tip: Sort craft materials by colour beforehand to speed up the rainbow craft.

Opening Prayer & Gathering

Welcome circle

Gather in a circle. Ask children about a promise someone kept for them, and how it felt.

Dear God, thank you that you always keep your promises. Help us to obey you like Noah did. Amen.

Icebreaker question

What is your favourite animal? If you were on the ark, which would you want next to you?

The Bible Story — Part 1

God Speaks to Noah

Noah was a good man who loved and obeyed God. But the world had become full of wrongdoing.

God told Noah, 'I will send a great flood. Build a big boat, an ark, to keep your family and the animals safe.'

The Bible Story — Part 2

Building the Ark

Noah obeyed. He and his sons worked for years to build the huge wooden ark, exactly as God said.

People laughed at Noah for building a boat on dry land, but Noah kept trusting and obeying God.

The Bible Story — Part 3

Two by Two

When the ark was ready, God sent the animals — two of every kind. They walked, hopped and flew inside.

Noah's family went in too. Then God shut the door, and the rain began to fall.

The Bible Story — Part 4

The Rainbow Promise

It rained forty days and nights, but the ark floated safely. At last the water went down and everyone came out.

God placed a beautiful rainbow in the sky and promised never again to flood the whole earth. God keeps his promises!

Talk About It

Discussion questions for the group

Sit in a circle. Let everyone share. There are no wrong answers when we talk about God's Word!

1. Why did Noah keep building even when people laughed?

2. How do you think Noah cared for all the animals?

3. What does the rainbow remind us about God?

4. When is it hard to obey?

5. How can we trust God's promises?

Living It Out

Obeying God like Noah

Read each situation. Circle the choice that pleases God. Talk about it together.

1. A parent asks you to do a chore you dislike.

a) Obey with a good attitude.   b) Complain and refuse.

2. Others tease you for doing right.

a) Stop to fit in.   b) Keep doing right, like Noah.

3. A friend feels left out.

a) Care for them and include them.   b) Ignore them.

Act It Out!

Retell the story with a simple drama

Teacher tip: Give children simple roles and props. Acting the story helps them remember it.

Roles & steps

  1. Choose Noah, his family, and children to be animals in pairs.
  2. Noah 'builds' the ark while others 'laugh', then wonder.
  3. Animals march in two by two.
  4. Everyone cheers under a paper rainbow at the end!

Game: Animals Two by Two

A fun game for the whole group

You need: pairs of animal cards or just imagination

How to play

  1. Whisper an animal name to pairs of children.
  2. On 'go', everyone makes their animal sound.
  3. Children must find their matching partner by sound.
  4. Once paired, they 'board the ark' (a taped square).
Teacher tip: Keep the noise fun but brief, then settle straight into the next activity.

Craft: Paper-Plate Rainbow

Make God's promise sign!

You need: paper or half a paper plate, crayons, cotton wool, glue
  1. Draw seven arches for the rainbow.
  2. Colour them red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
  3. Glue cotton-wool clouds at each end.
  4. Remember: the rainbow shows God keeps his promises!
