Lesson Aim & Teacher Preparation

Lesson aim: Children will learn that God can use anyone who is brave and trusts him to do what is right.

Learning objectives

  • Retell the main events of Esther's story.
  • Explain what it means to be brave for God.
  • Recite the memory verse (Esther 4:14).
  • Name one way to be brave and kind this week.

You will need

  • Bibles or the story pages printed
  • Crayons, pencils, scissors, glue
  • Card or paper for the crown craft
  • Optional: a toy crown or sticker jewels
Teacher tip: Set out crayons and craft supplies before class so transitions are smooth.

The Bible Story — Part 1

A New Queen

Long ago in the land of Persia, there was a great king named Xerxes. A young Jewish woman named Esther was chosen to be his queen. She was kind and beautiful.

Esther had been raised by her older cousin Mordecai, who loved her like his own daughter. He told her to keep it a secret that she was Jewish, for now.

The Bible Story — Part 2

A Wicked Plan

The king had a proud helper named Haman. Haman was angry because Mordecai would only bow to God, not to him.

So Haman made a terrible plan and tricked the king into signing a law that all the Jewish people would be destroyed on one day.

The Bible Story — Part 3

A Brave Choice

Mordecai sent word to Esther: 'You must go to the king and beg him to save our people!' But going to the king uninvited could mean death.

Mordecai said, 'Maybe you became queen for a time just like this.' Esther prayed and fasted, then made her brave choice: 'I will go to the king. If I die, I die.'

The Bible Story — Part 4

God Saves His People

Esther bravely told the king the truth about Haman's wicked plan. The king was angry — not at Esther, but at Haman!

Because Esther was brave, the king stopped the plan and the Jewish people were saved. God used one brave person to do something great.

Talk About It

Discussion questions for the group

Sit in a circle. Let everyone share. There are no wrong answers when we talk about God's Word!

1. Why was Esther afraid to go to the king?

2. What did Mordecai say that helped Esther be brave?

3. Why did Esther pray and fast before she acted?

4. When is it hard for you to do the right thing?

5. How can we ask God to help us be brave?

Act It Out!

Retell the story with a simple drama

Teacher tip: Give children simple roles and props. Acting the story helps them remember it.

Roles & steps

  1. Choose children to play Esther, Mordecai, the King and Haman.
  2. Narrator reads the story slowly while actors mime the actions.
  3. Esther bravely 'speaks' to the King to save her people.
  4. End with everyone cheering because God saved the day!

Game: Brave Crown Relay

A fun game for the whole group

You need: two paper crowns, two chairs

How to play

  1. Split into two teams.
  2. Each child wears the crown, walks to the chair and back.
  3. They pass the crown to the next player.
  4. The first team to finish shouts 'Be brave!'
Teacher tip: Cheer for every child so being 'brave' up front feels safe and fun.

Craft: Make a Paper Crown

You need: card or paper, scissors, crayons, glue, sticker jewels
